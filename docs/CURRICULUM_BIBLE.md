# English Journey Curriculum Bible

**Status:** Governing source of truth for the A1–C2, 865-lesson re-architecture.  
**Course principle:** predictable in navigation, unpredictable in activity; calm in presentation, active in learning.

## Production protocol

| Order | Specialist role | Required output | May not silently do |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Researcher | Evidence record: source, principle, application, risk, level fit, priority | Architect or write final lesson content |
| 2 | Curriculum Architect | Level/module arc and lesson blueprints | Write final content or define interface design |
| 3 | Content Creator | Bilingual activities, examples, prompts, scripts, mentor copy | Change approved objectives |
| 4 | Pedagogical QA | `PASS`, `PASS WITH CHANGES`, or `FAIL`; problem, evidence, severity, required change | Silently rewrite a failed lesson |
| 5 | Learning Experience + Visual Designer | Semantic visual/disclosure review | Redesign pedagogy or assessment objective |
| 6 | Assessment Architect | Lesson/module/level assessment alignment | Replace the curriculum architecture |

No lesson is complete until all relevant handoffs are recorded. A downstream problem returns to the specialist who owns its cause.

## Lesson blueprint contract

Every lesson records: CEFR level; module; number; can-do outcome; dominant archetype; primary/secondary skill; density; time; new language; retrieval; prerequisites; later connection; cognitive-load decision; activity pattern; Arabic-support rationale; first-view guidance; and assessment evidence.

> **Outcome test:** What can the learner do reliably after this lesson that they could not reliably do before?

## Archetypes and density

| Archetype | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Discover | Meet a meaningful new idea or context. |
| Notice | Detect a form, contrast, pattern, or implication. |
| Reading | Understand, infer, evaluate, or use a text. |
| Writing | Produce, revise, or communicate for a purpose. |
| Speaking | Rehearse or perform understandable spoken language. |
| Listening | Extract detail, intention, sequence, attitude, or inference. |
| Grammar | Clarify a language choice in context. |
| Vocabulary | Build useful word knowledge through recognition and use. |
| Interaction | Respond, negotiate, repair, collaborate, or mediate. |
| Real World | Use authentic artefacts, constraints, and communication. |
| Integration | Combine skills/language toward a purposeful outcome. |
| Review | Retrieve and reconnect prior learning. |
| Assessment | Demonstrate meaningful transfer. |

Learning stages are not a page template. A lesson may omit a stage when its objective does not need it. Use **light 10–15**, **normal 15–25**, and **deep 25–40 minute** lessons deliberately; a module needs an irregular but coherent rhythm.

## CEFR progression

| Band | Primary growth | Support rule |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Recognition, supported use, concrete familiar contexts | Short direct instructions; Arabic only where it removes a real barrier; one clear next step. |
| A2 | Connected personal/community communication and guided independence | Longer connections and mentor bridges; retain high support. |
| B1 | Independent communication, source awareness, explanation, opinion | Add qualified claims, practical tasks, and connected production. |
| B2 | Nuance, mediation, evidence, register, justified choice | Reduce scaffolding; expect comparison and transfer. |
| C1 | Analysis, persuasion, strategic language, sustained discourse | Use complex source work, revision, and audience awareness. |
| C2 | Precision, flexible synthesis, expert register, reflective transfer | Assess ambiguity, synthesis, stance, and high-stakes performance. |

## Visual language and progressive disclosure

Tasks use **semantic colour + recognisable icon + visible text label**. Colour is never the only signal. Keep a neutral canvas, restrained accents, readable line lengths, clear focus states, and accessible contrast.

| Treatment | Must make visible |
|---|---|
| Orientation | What this is; what to do; what matters; what comes next. |
| Explanation | Key idea, example, grammar, word meaning. |
| Support | Tip, Arabic help, worked example, transcript, common mistake. |
| Action | Type label, clear instruction, response area, time expectation. |
| Retrieval | Prior-learning cue and reason for its return. |
| Assessment | Evidence requested, criteria, feedback/next bridge. |

Show the current action and enough help to begin. Put optional examples, translations, explanations, and transcripts behind intentional disclosure; never hide a required instruction.

## Activity, retrieval, and assessment

Reuse language by changing its purpose: introduce → recognise → understand → use → retrieve → contrast → independent use. Reject generic `words → dialogue → five questions` patterns unless uniquely appropriate.

Assessment is graph-aware: 3–5 item lesson mini-checks; 8–15 item checkpoints each 10 lessons; 20–30 item milestones each 30 lessons; integrated module assessment; and CEFR-level challenge. Use contextual choice, transformation, correction, inference, evidence, and production rubrics. Do not test misspellings as distractors or use copied sentences as proof of mastery.

## Required quality gates

| Gate | Reject when |
|---|---|
| Architecture | Outcome unclear, CEFR mismatch, missing prerequisites/future connection, or no meaningful module arc. |
| Content | Unnatural English, misleading/excessive Arabic, purposeless task, filler, or pedagogically unjustified duplication. |
| Pedagogical QA | Excessive load, nearby route repetition, artificial retrieval, or evidence cannot prove the outcome. |
| Visual QA | Meaning relies on colour/icon/position; disclosure overwhelms; hierarchy, contrast, focus, or keyboard needs fail. |
| Assessment QA | Repetitive question patterns, needlessly difficult wording, production reduced to MCQ, or no curriculum-graph coverage. |
| Engineering QA | Types, integrity suite, learner route, build, audit, and visual verification do not pass. |

## Explicit exclusions

No universal lesson template; no forced skill rotation; no filler for visual fullness; no fake testimonials; no arbitrary Arabic translation; no forced retrieval; and no per-request external AI requirement.
