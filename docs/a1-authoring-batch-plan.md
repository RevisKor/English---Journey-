# A1 Full Authoring Batch Plan

> **Scope:** This is the production plan for genuinely reauthoring the active **90-lesson A1 course**. It does not describe a generic template and does not certify content as complete until the live lesson data, assessment banks, learner route, and QA evidence all agree.

## 1. Production Unit

The unit of completion is **one 15-lesson module**, not a document, a scaffold, or a collection of vocabulary lists. Every module is authored as a connected experience with a clear communicative destination. The module then receives an independent quality review before the next module becomes eligible for release.

| Module batch | Lesson range | Communicative destination | Production status |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A1.1 · Meeting People | 01–15 | A supported first conversation: greeting, name, age, politeness, and closing. | **In authoring.** Lessons 1–5 are the approved archetype and visual-language pilot; they still require final live-content verification. |
| A1.2 · Family and Home | 16–30 | Describe people, possessions, rooms, and simple locations. | Queued |
| A1.3 · Food and Markets | 31–45 | Name food, express a preference, and manage a small market exchange. | Queued |
| A1.4 · Daily Life | 46–60 | Describe time, routines, and simple responsibilities. | Queued |
| A1.5 · Places and Getting Around | 61–75 | Identify places, ask for help, and follow or give a short route. | Queued |
| A1.6 · Work, Hobbies, and Real Interactions | 76–90 | Exchange basic connected information about work, hobbies, preferences, and plans. | Queued |

## 2. What Must Be Authored Per Lesson

The six workstreams share one lesson record. They contribute to their defined fields and do not overwrite each other’s work.

| Workstream | Required live evidence | Approval question |
|---|---|---|
| Curriculum architect | Objective, prerequisites, natural retrieval link, forward link, archetype, density, and selected learning stages. | Does this lesson do one worthwhile thing at the right point in the module arc? |
| Language-learning specialist | Beginner-safe explanation, Arabic support, controlled examples, Arabic-speaker risk note, and a realistic mastery expectation. | Can a learner with no grammar terminology understand what to do and why? |
| Activity and UX designer | Specific task instructions, only the needed stages, word-only speech actions, transitions, and progressive support. | Is the next action obvious without making the lesson mechanically uniform? |
| Assessment designer | Contextual question-bank items, valid distractors, feedback intent, a justified assessment role, and module-test mapping. | Does the evidence test language use rather than spelling traps or answer-position guessing? |
| Visual-language designer | Semantic card choice, icon/label, optional visual or scene brief, caption/alt-text direction, and disclosure decision. | Does the interface clarify meaning or action while remaining calm? |
| QA and research reviewer | CEFR check, Arabic clarity check, repeated-exposure evidence, assessment validity, data validation, and audit outcome. | Is there a blocker, an unresolved ambiguity, or an avoidable repetition? |

## 3. A1.1 Authoring Waves

The first module is deliberately divided into short integration waves. A wave is reviewable, but **only the complete 15-lesson module can be recorded as released**.

| Wave | Lessons | Dominant experiences | Required authored evidence | Gate |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01–05 | Discover, Interaction, Grammar Notice, Vocabulary, Speaking | First-contact language; selected archetypes; controlled dialogue; word-only pronunciation; first retrieval link. | Five lesson records visibly differ in stages, openings, activity shape, and density. |
| 2 | 06–08 | Discover, Vocabulary, Real World | Personal information and classroom language; numbers, colours, objects, instructions; natural retrieval of greetings and identity language. | No old word appears merely to satisfy a retrieval quota. |
| 3 | 09–11 | Reading, Writing, Interaction | First connected reading; profile-card writing; third-person supported conversation. | Reading and writing exist because the module is ready for them, not because every lesson must include them. |
| 4 | 12–15 | Review, Integration, Assessment | Contextual review, first conversation rehearsal, synthesis, and module checkpoint. | Assessment items cover taught communication and grammar with no repeated question format dominance. |

## 4. Archetype and Density Rule

There is **no universal order** and no mandatory skill rotation. The curriculum architect selects the dominant experience after considering the objective, prior learning, learner load, and the next lesson. The audit provides review signals, not quotas that force an unnatural lesson.

| Authoring decision | A1 application |
|---|---|
| Light lesson | One focused discovery, notice, or retrieval task. Useful after a deep lesson or when automaticity is the priority. |
| Normal lesson | A clear objective plus two or three connected experiences, such as vocabulary → supported use or interaction → retrieval. |
| Deep lesson | A carefully supported integration, reading, writing, review, or assessment task. It earns its extra load through meaningful use of already prepared language. |
| No-new-language lesson | A deliberate use, review, conversation rehearsal, or assessment lesson that asks learners to mobilise existing knowledge. |
| Retrieval | A natural prerequisite or communicative bridge with a source lesson recorded. Retrieval is omitted when it would create an artificial sentence. |

## 5. Module Acceptance Criteria

The A1.1 module is ready for a checkpoint only when every criterion below is true.

| Area | Required condition |
|---|---|
| Content | All 15 live lessons have complete objective, mentor, language, activity, and bridge content—not generic placeholders. |
| Variation | The module contains purposeful archetype and density variation; review signals show no unexamined repeated opening, activity sequence, dialogue shape, or assessment format. |
| Repeated exposure | Each core item returns in a natural later use, dialogue, reading, writing, or review context, with provenance recorded. |
| Arabic support | Explanations are understandable to A1 Arabic speakers; Arabic clarifies rather than replaces English practice. |
| Assessments | Every item belongs to the taught scope, uses contextual options, and maps to an evidence claim. The module test synthesises rather than copies lesson questions. |
| Learner route | The workspace shows **What is this? → What do I do? → What matters? → What is next?** and renders only the selected stages. |
| Visual language | Objectives, examples, tips, common mistakes, vocabulary, grammar, activities, retrieval, and assessment use text labels, icons, and semantic styling—not colour alone. |
| Validation | TypeScript, relevant unit tests, production build, and curriculum audit pass. Open review signals have documented editorial decisions. |

## 6. Evidence and Versioning

Each completed module creates a small evidence bundle: the module graph, lesson records, question-bank mapping, change log, audit report, focused tests, and a reviewer sign-off. This keeps the course editable and prevents the 90-lesson A1 rewrite from being confused with finished work because a shared component or planning file exists.
