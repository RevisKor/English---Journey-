# A1 Curriculum Research Notes

## Council of Europe CEFR descriptors

The Council of Europe states that the CEFR provides a common basis for language syllabuses, curriculum guidelines, examinations, and textbooks. It describes what learners need to learn to do in communication and the knowledge and skills needed to act effectively. The official A1 descriptors include understanding everyday expressions for concrete needs when speech is clear, slow, and repeated; understanding carefully delivered questions and short directions; using a very basic range of expressions about personal details and concrete needs; and using some basic one-clause structures, with omissions or reductions possible. Source: [Council of Europe CEFR descriptors search](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search).

The CEFR descriptor page also exposes separate scales for spoken interaction, online interaction, written production, mediation, strategies, and linguistic competence. For an A1 product, this supports a multi-skill route rather than a grammar-only sequence, but the tasks must remain concrete, highly scaffolded, and short.

## Cambridge A1-aligned evidence

The Cambridge Pre A1 Starters, A1 Movers and A2 Flyers handbook describes a progression built around listening, speaking, reading, and writing, with thematic vocabulary and explicit grammar-and-structures lists. For A1, the handbook gives examples such as understanding very simple dialogues and descriptions on familiar topics, responding to familiar questions with simple phrases and sentences, giving simple descriptions, asking about habits and preferences, reading short factual texts and simple familiar stories with picture support, and writing short phrases and sentences about personal details, pictures, likes, and dislikes. Source: [Cambridge handbook for teachers, 2024](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/357180-starters-movers-and-flyers-handbook-for-teachers-2024.pdf).

The handbook states that vocabulary can be revised and reinforced, and presents positive, skill-specific progress reporting rather than treating a single pass/fail event as the entire learning signal. This supports repeated exposure, mixed practice, and module-level skill profiles in English Journey.

## Design implications for English Journey

The A1 route should begin below ordinary classroom terminology: each grammar explanation must first show meaning and use in a concrete situation, then name the pattern only if useful. Activities should move from recognition to controlled use to supported production. Each module should repeatedly retrieve vocabulary through visual recognition, pronunciation, dialogue, reading, writing, contextual selection, and review. The active catalog must contain the authored lesson records; a preview-only blueprint is insufficient for the learner route.

The proposed A1 architecture is six thematic modules with 15 lessons each, for 90 active lessons. Every module follows a repeated rhythm: orientation and vocabulary, picture/category consolidation, grammar in context, controlled practice, dialogue/interaction, speaking repetition, reading, writing, retrieval review, milestone checkpoint, and a module test. Exact lesson recipes are specified in the A1 review document.
