# Curriculum design research

## Design decisions

English Journey will use an action-oriented, outcome-led route rather than disconnected grammar pages. The CEFR Companion Volume organizes language work across reception, production, interaction, mediation, plurilingual/pluricultural competence, and communicative language competences. For C1 and C2, this means lessons must repeatedly ask learners to interpret arguments, produce sustained language, interact around a purpose, and mediate or reshape information—not only answer vocabulary questions.

C1 modules will include varied text types and explicit work on collocation, shades of meaning, grammar control, argument structure, register, and audience. The lesson sequence will alternate input, noticing, controlled practice, contextual retrieval, reading/listening-style comprehension, and purposeful writing. C2 will add implication, stance, rhetorical effect, stylistic choice, source comparison, and mediation of dense or culturally situated ideas.

The course architecture will use named modules with a clear communicative domain. Each module will contain a sequence of vocabulary-centered lessons; grammar, pronunciation, reading, writing, interaction, and mediation will be distributed across the sequence rather than forced into every lesson. Milestone checkpoints will sample the module's accumulated language, while cumulative module tests remain gated until all module lessons are completed.

Arabic will be scaffolding, not a replacement for the target language. A1 and A2 will show more Arabic in introductions, explanations, transitions, hints, and assessment review. B1 will reduce but retain Arabic for difficult concepts and feedback. B2–C2 will remain English-first with concise Arabic support available where it reduces ambiguity.

## Reference observations

The Council of Europe Companion Volume (2020) updates the CEFR and includes descriptors for reception, production, interaction, mediation, online interaction, plurilingual/pluricultural competence, vocabulary range, grammatical accuracy, phonological control, orthographic control, sociolinguistic appropriateness, coherence, cohesion, precision, and fluency. These dimensions will be represented in the C1/C2 learning outcomes rather than treating level as vocabulary difficulty alone.

Cambridge C1 Advanced describes an integrated assessment model covering Reading and Use of English, Writing, Listening, and Speaking. Its Reading and Use of English specification includes collocations, idioms, phrasal verbs, grammar, word formation, transformations, reading for detail, opinion, tone, purpose, implication, cross-text comparison, and text organization. Its writing tasks include essays and genre variation such as reports, proposals, reviews, and letters or emails. English Journey will adapt these as practice strands, not reproduce the examination.

Oxford Online English presents lessons as focused communicative topics with vocabulary in context, dialogues or listening scripts, grammar, pronunciation, writing, speaking, and quizzes. Its archive includes everyday domains at lower levels and workplace, academic, exam, and abstract topics at higher levels. This supports the proposed thematic module model.

## Application to this project

The current external-AI prompt strategy remains unchanged. Prompts should expose the lesson outcome, target vocabulary, grammar focus, level, genre, and bilingual support instructions. Question banks should test contextual meaning, collocation, grammar-in-context, discourse organization, and retrieval. The database catalog should preserve level, module, lesson, topic, and question-bank scope so personalized attempts remain possible after restructuring.
