# English Journey — Fully Authored Sample: A1 Lessons 1–5

**Purpose.** This is a review draft showing the intended **lesson-level depth and learner experience** for the first five A1 lessons. It is not a claim that the remaining 85 A1 lessons have already been rewritten. It is also **not a universal lesson template**. Each lesson chooses the smallest set of learning stages that serves its objective: a first greeting can be discovered through a short social moment, a grammar hinge can need deeper contrast and construction, and an integration lesson can deliberately introduce nothing new.

The sample deliberately keeps new language small and useful, because early learners need clear goals, worked models, and manageable novelty.[1] The same language then returns in a new task rather than appearing only once, so that retrieval becomes meaningful rather than a disconnected memory test.[2] Each lesson ends with observable A1-style evidence: the learner can understand, produce, or interact with a short message for a real everyday purpose.[3]

> **Learner promise / وعد للمتعلم:** You do not need to understand all English today. You only need to understand and use **one small piece** well. We will meet it again later.
>
> لا تحتاج إلى فهم كل الإنجليزية اليوم. تحتاج فقط إلى فهم واستخدام **جزء صغير واحد** بشكل جيد. سنقابله مرة أخرى لاحقاً.

## Predictable navigation, varied lesson experiences

The learner does not choose disconnected tabs called “vocabulary”, “grammar”, or “quiz”. Every lesson opens with a stable four-part orientation: **what this is, what to do, why it matters, and what comes next**. What changes is the actual learning experience. Every word card has a **word-only British pronunciation button**, an Arabic meaning, a short example, and a reason for meeting the word now. A learner can optionally copy a prepared prompt to an external AI service for extra explanation or writing feedback; English Journey itself makes no paid AI request.

| Lesson | Archetype and density | Chosen experience | Stages intentionally not forced |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A First Hello | **Discover** · Normal | A first social encounter, word exploration, listen-and-repeat, and short choice. | No formal grammar lesson and no writing task. |
| 2. My Name Is… | **Interaction** · Light | A name exchange begins immediately; vocabulary and my/your are noticed inside turns. | No long grammar explanation or separate reading passage. |
| 3. I Am / You Are | **Grammar** · Deep | A contrast with Arabic, worked model, construction practice, and one personal sentence ladder. | No extra topic vocabulary or full dialogue performance. |
| 4. People Around Us | **Visual Vocabulary** · Light | Picture-led noticing, audio-to-image matching, and a small caption-building task. | No extended mentor preface or independent writing. |
| 5. Listen, Repeat, Meet | **Speaking Integration** · Normal | A supported role-play that reuses the previous four lessons, with self-check evidence. | No major new grammar, word list, or multiple-choice end quiz. |

The same semantic activity markers appear throughout: **Your goal**, **Example**, **Helpful tip**, **Watch out**, **Words to use**, **Notice the pattern**, **Try it**, **Remember**, and **Check your progress**. Each marker combines a text label, an icon, and a restrained accent; no learner has to infer meaning from colour alone.

# Lesson 1 — A First Hello

| Lesson identity | English | العربية |
|---|---|---|
| Module | **Meeting People: Your First English Community** | **التعرّف إلى الناس: مجتمعك الأول باللغة الإنجليزية** |
| Time | 40 minutes, with permission to stop after any step and return later. | 40 دقيقة، ويمكنك التوقف بعد أي خطوة والعودة لاحقاً. |
| Can-do outcome | **I can greet someone, say goodbye, and use “please” and “thank you” politely.** | **أستطيع أن أحيّي شخصاً، وأقول وداعاً، وأستخدم please وthank you بأدب.** |
| Situation | You meet a new classmate at the beginning and end of a short class. | تقابل زميلاً جديداً في بداية حصة قصيرة ونهايتها. |
| Success evidence | The learner completes a two-line greeting and chooses an appropriate greeting or closing in context. | يُكمل المتعلم تحية من سطرين ويختار تحية أو وداعاً مناسباً في السياق. |

## 1. Start together — your first success

> **Mentor:** Welcome. Today, you do not need to make a long sentence. You will learn a few words that let you open and close a friendly conversation. That is real English already.
>
> **المرشد:** أهلاً بك. اليوم لا تحتاج إلى تكوين جملة طويلة. ستتعلم كلمات قليلة تساعدك على بدء محادثة ودية وإنهائها. وهذا إنجليزي حقيقي بالفعل.

**Tiny target / هدف صغير:** By the end, say: **“Hello.” — “Hello.” — “Thank you. Goodbye.”**

## 2. Meet the useful words

| Word | Arabic | British sound | Meaning and use | Model sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **hello** | مرحباً | /həˈləʊ/ | A friendly greeting. Use it when you meet someone. | **Hello, Sara.** |
| **hi** | أهلاً / مرحباً | /haɪ/ | An informal, friendly greeting. | **Hi, Ali.** |
| **goodbye** | إلى اللقاء | /ɡʊdˈbaɪ/ | A polite closing when you leave. | **Goodbye, Omar.** |
| **thank you** | شكراً | /ˈθæŋk juː/ | Say this when someone helps you or gives you something. | **Thank you for the book.** |
| **please** | من فضلك | /pliːz/ | Makes a request polite. | **A pen, please.** |

### Word-only pronunciation practice

For each card, the learner presses the sound button and hears **only the word or phrase**, not the entire example sentence. The interface displays this practice sequence:

| Listen | Say it slowly | Say it naturally |
|---|---|---|
| `hello` | hel–**LO** | **Hello!** |
| `goodbye` | good–**BYE** | **Goodbye!** |
| `thank you` | **THANK** you | **Thank you.** |

**Arabic speaker note / ملاحظة للمتحدث بالعربية:** In **thank**, put your tongue gently between your teeth for **/θ/**. Do not worry if it is not perfect on the first try. Make the air come out softly: **th… thank**.

## 3. Notice one small pattern — words make a message

**English explanation.** A sentence is a group of words that gives a complete message. English usually starts a sentence with a capital letter and ends with a mark. Today, even a one-word greeting can be a complete message:

> **Hello.**  
> **Goodbye.**  
> **Thank you.**

**الشرح بالعربية.** الجملة هي مجموعة كلمات تعطي رسالة كاملة. تبدأ الجملة الإنجليزية عادة بحرف كبير وتنتهي بعلامة. واليوم يمكن أن تكون التحية من كلمة واحدة رسالة كاملة:

> **Hello.** = مرحباً.  
> **Goodbye.** = إلى اللقاء.  
> **Thank you.** = شكراً.

**Notice, not memorise / لاحظ ولا تحفظ فقط:** `Hello` is for meeting. `Goodbye` is for leaving. `Thank you` is for responding politely after help.

## 4. Hear and say

**Listen once for the situation.**

> **Maya:** Hello!  
> **Adam:** Hi!  
> **Maya:** Here is your book.  
> **Adam:** Thank you.  
> **Maya:** Goodbye!  
> **Adam:** Goodbye!

**Arabic support / دعم بالعربية:**

> **مايا:** مرحباً!  
> **آدم:** أهلاً!  
> **مايا:** هذا كتابك.  
> **آدم:** شكراً.  
> **مايا:** إلى اللقاء!  
> **آدم:** إلى اللقاء!

**Repeat-after-me routine.** The learner hears one line, sees a three-second pause, repeats it, then checks the line. The programme explicitly accepts a quiet attempt, a whisper, or speaking aloud.

1. Say: **Hello!**
2. Say: **Hi!**
3. Say: **Thank you.**
4. Say: **Goodbye!**

## 5. Use it in life

### Guided choice

Choose the best phrase. Then read the complete mini-conversation aloud.

| Situation | Choose | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| You enter your English class at 9:00. | **Hello** / Goodbye / Thank you | **Hello** is for meeting someone. |
| Your teacher gives you a pen. | Hello / Goodbye / **Thank you** | **Thank you** is a polite response to help. |
| You leave class at the end. | Hello / **Goodbye** / Please | **Goodbye** closes the conversation. |

### My turn

Choose one name: **Sara**, **Omar**, or **your own name**. Say or type:

> **Hello, ____. Thank you. Goodbye.**

## 6. Remember and check

**Look away from the cards.** Which word would you use when you leave? Say it before revealing the answer.

<details>
<summary>Reveal answer</summary>

**Goodbye.**
</details>

### Lesson check

1. You arrive at a friend’s home. Which response fits best?
   - A. Goodbye
   - B. **Hello**
   - C. Thank you

2. A classmate gives you your notebook. What do you say?
   - A. Hello
   - B. Goodbye
   - C. **Thank you**

3. Put the message in the best order for the end of a short meeting.
   - A. Hello, thank you.
   - B. **Thank you. Goodbye.**
   - C. Goodbye, please.

**Feedback if not yet correct.** “Look at the situation first: are you meeting, receiving help, or leaving? The situation tells you which phrase you need.”

## Retrieval link to Lesson 2

At the beginning of Lesson 2, the learner sees: **“It is morning. You meet a new person. Which greeting can you use: hello, goodbye, or thank you?”** The correct answer becomes the opening word of the next conversation.

# Lesson 2 — My Name Is…

| Lesson identity | English | العربية |
|---|---|---|
| Can-do outcome | **I can say my name and ask another person’s name.** | **أستطيع أن أقول اسمي وأسأل عن اسم شخص آخر.** |
| New language | **name, my, your, I, you** | **الاسم، خاصتي، خاصتك، أنا، أنت** |
| Retrieval from Lesson 1 | Begin the exchange with **Hello** and finish with **Thank you / Goodbye**. | نبدأ الحوار بـ **Hello** وننهيه بـ **Thank you / Goodbye**. |
| Success evidence | The learner completes a four-line name exchange with correct **my** and **your**. | يُكمل المتعلم حواراً من أربعة أسطر عن الاسم باستخدام **my** و**your** بشكل صحيح. |

## 1. Start together

> **Mentor:** Yesterday you could open a conversation. Today you will add the next useful piece: your name. A conversation does not need to be long to be real.
>
> **المرشد:** بالأمس استطعت أن تبدأ محادثة. اليوم ستضيف الجزء المفيد التالي: اسمك. لا تحتاج المحادثة إلى أن تكون طويلة حتى تكون حقيقية.

**Warm retrieval / استرجاع سريع:** You meet someone. Say one greeting from Lesson 1: **Hello** or **Hi**.

## 2. Meet the useful words

| Word | Arabic | British sound | Use it like this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **name** | اسم | /neɪm/ | **My name is Noor.** |
| **my** | لي / خاصتي | /maɪ/ | **my name** = اسمي |
| **your** | لك / خاصتك | /jɔː/ | **your name** = اسمك |
| **I** | أنا | /aɪ/ | **I am Noor.** |
| **you** | أنت | /juː/ | **You are Ali.** |

### Meaning in a real exchange

| English | Arabic | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **My name is Noor.** | اسمي نور. | I am telling you my name. |
| **What is your name?** | ما اسمك؟ | I am asking you for your name. |

## 3. Notice one small pattern — my and your

**English explanation.** Use **my** before something that belongs to you: **my name**. Use **your** before something that belongs to the other person: **your name**. In this lesson, do not learn a long rule. Just notice the pair:

> **My** name is Noor.  
> What is **your** name?

**الشرح بالعربية.** نستخدم **my** قبل الشيء الذي يخصني: **my name** = اسمي. ونستخدم **your** قبل الشيء الذي يخص الشخص الآخر: **your name** = اسمك.

| Who is speaking? | Correct phrase | Why? |
|---|---|---|
| I am talking about myself. | **my name** | الاسم يخصني. |
| I am asking another person. | **your name** | الاسم يخص الشخص الآخر. |

**Common Arabic-speaker risk / خطأ شائع:** Do not say **“What your name?”** in a complete English question. We need the small word **is**: **What is your name?** Lesson 3 will explain why English needs this word.

## 4. Hear and say

> **Noor:** Hello. My name is Noor. What is your name?  
> **Ali:** Hi. My name is Ali.  
> **Noor:** Thank you, Ali.  
> **Ali:** Goodbye, Noor.

### Echo practice

Read the first half, pause, then answer as the other person.

| Prompt | Your answer frame |
|---|---|
| **Hello. My name is Noor. What is your name?** | **Hi. My name is ____.** |
| **Thank you, ____.** | **Goodbye, ____.** |

## 5. Use it in life

### Build the conversation

Put the lines in a natural order.

1. `My name is Hana.`
2. `Hello. What is your name?`
3. `Thank you, Hana. Goodbye.`
4. `Hi. My name is Karim.`

<details>
<summary>Suggested order</summary>

**2 → 4 → 1 → 3.** A natural version is: “Hello. What is your name?” “Hi. My name is Karim.” “My name is Hana.” “Thank you, Hana. Goodbye.”
</details>

### Supported speaking or writing

Use this frame. Change only the name first; then, if you feel ready, say it without looking.

> **Hello. My name is ____. What is your name?**

## 6. Remember and check

1. Complete the question: `What is ___ name?`
   - A. my
   - B. **your**
   - C. I

2. Complete the statement: `___ name is Fatima.`
   - A. **My**
   - B. Your
   - C. You

3. Choose the reply that gives a name.
   - A. Goodbye.
   - B. **My name is Sami.**
   - C. Thank you.

**External AI practice prompt, optional and free to the platform.**

> I am an A1 English learner whose first language is Arabic. Practise a very short English conversation with me. Ask my name, wait for my answer, then tell me your name. Use only simple words: hello, hi, my, your, name, thank you, goodbye. Correct only one important mistake at a time, and explain it briefly in Arabic.

## Retrieval link to Lesson 3

The next lesson starts with a sentence the learner already knows: **My name is Noor.** The learner will discover why the word **is** is necessary.

# Lesson 3 — I Am / You Are

| Lesson identity | English | العربية |
|---|---|---|
| Can-do outcome | **I can make a simple sentence about myself or another person using I am / you are.** | **أستطيع أن أكون جملة بسيطة عن نفسي أو عن شخص آخر باستخدام I am / you are.** |
| New language | **am, are, student, friend, happy** | **am، are، طالب، صديق، سعيد** |
| Retrieval | Reuse **I, you, my, your, name** in identity sentences. | نعيد استخدام **I وyou وmy وyour وname** في جمل التعريف. |
| Success evidence | The learner gives two true or possible sentences: one with **I am**, one with **You are**. | يُنتج المتعلم جملتين صحيحتين: واحدة مع **I am** وواحدة مع **You are**. |

## 1. Start together

> **Mentor:** You already say, “My name is…”. Now you will use another tiny English engine: **am** and **are**. They help English connect a person to information about that person.
>
> **المرشد:** أنت تقول بالفعل “My name is…”. الآن ستستخدم محركاً صغيراً آخر في الإنجليزية: **am** و**are**. تساعدان الإنجليزية على ربط الشخص بمعلومة عنه.

**Warm retrieval:** Say: **My name is ____.** Find the small word before your name: **is**.

## 2. Meet the useful words

| Word or phrase | Arabic | British sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I am** | أنا أكون / أنا | /aɪ æm/ | **I am a student.** |
| **you are** | أنت تكون / أنت | /juː ɑː/ | **You are my friend.** |
| **student** | طالب / طالبة | /ˈstjuːdnt/ | **I am a student.** |
| **friend** | صديق / صديقة | /frend/ | **You are my friend.** |
| **happy** | سعيد / سعيدة | /ˈhæpi/ | **I am happy.** |

## 3. Notice one small pattern — English needs am or are

**English explanation.** In Arabic, you can say:

> أنا طالب.

The meaning is complete. In English, we usually need a small word between the person and the information:

> **I am a student.**

Use **am** with **I**. Use **are** with **you**.

| Person | Small English word | Complete example |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **am** | **I am happy.** |
| **you** | **are** | **You are my friend.** |

**الشرح بالعربية.** في العربية نقول: **أنا طالب**، والجملة واضحة من دون فعل ظاهر في المضارع. في الإنجليزية نحتاج غالباً إلى كلمة صغيرة بين الشخص والمعلومة:

> **I am a student.** = أنا طالب / طالبة.

نستخدم **am** مع **I**، ونستخدم **are** مع **you**.

**Do not say / لا تقل:** `I student.`  
**Say / قل:** `I am a student.`

## 4. Hear and say

> **Lina:** Hello. I am Lina. I am a student.  
> **Yusuf:** Hi, Lina. I am Yusuf. You are my friend.  
> **Lina:** Thank you. I am happy.

### Say it in parts

| Build | Say the full sentence |
|---|---|
| I + am + happy | **I am happy.** |
| You + are + my friend | **You are my friend.** |
| I + am + a student | **I am a student.** |

**Pronunciation note:** In natural speech, **I am** can sound close together: `/aɪæm/`. Keep both words; do not remove **am**.

## 5. Use it in life

### Meaning before form

Read the cards. Select the sentence that matches each card.

| Meaning | Correct English sentence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| أنا سعيد. | **I am happy.** | **I** needs **am**. |
| أنت صديقي. | **You are my friend.** | **You** needs **are**. |
| أنا طالب. | **I am a student.** | English connects the person and information with **am**. |

### My sentence ladder

Say or write one sentence on each step. The learner may choose an imaginary identity if privacy is preferred.

1. **I am ____.**
2. **I am a ____.**
3. **You are my ____.**

## 6. Remember and check

1. Choose the complete sentence.
   - A. I a student.
   - B. **I am a student.**
   - C. I are a student.

2. Complete: `You ___ my friend.`
   - A. am
   - B. **are**
   - C. is

3. Choose the sentence that means **أنا سعيد**.
   - A. You are happy.
   - B. **I am happy.**
   - C. I am friend.

**Feedback if needed.** “First choose the person: **I** or **you**. Then choose its partner: **I am**; **you are**.”

## Retrieval link to Lesson 4

Lesson 4 introduces people words. The learner returns to today’s pattern to say: **This is a woman. She is a friend.** The programme first reminds them: **I am** and **you are** are two ways English connects a person to information.

# Lesson 4 — People Around Us

| Lesson identity | English | العربية |
|---|---|---|
| Can-do outcome | **I can identify one person in a picture and make a short sentence about that person.** | **أستطيع أن أحدد شخصاً واحداً في صورة وأن أكون جملة قصيرة عنه.** |
| New language | **man, woman, child, boy, girl, friend** | **رجل، امرأة، طفل، ولد، بنت، صديق** |
| Grammar focus | One person: **a** + singular person word. | شخص واحد: **a** + اسم شخص مفرد. |
| Visual brief | Six simple, culturally neutral illustrated people; each card has an accessible English label and Arabic support that can be shown or hidden. | ست صور توضيحية بسيطة ومحايدة ثقافياً لأشخاص؛ لكل بطاقة تسمية إنجليزية ودعم عربي يمكن إظهاره أو إخفاؤه. |

## 1. Start together

> **Mentor:** Today, pictures help us connect English to real people. You will not learn every people word in the world. You will learn a small set, then use it to make clear sentences.
>
> **المرشد:** اليوم تساعدنا الصور على ربط الإنجليزية بأشخاص حقيقيين. لن تتعلم كل كلمات الأشخاص في العالم. ستتعلم مجموعة صغيرة، ثم تستخدمها لتكوين جمل واضحة.

**Warm retrieval:** Say one true sentence from Lesson 3: **I am happy**, **I am a student**, or another short sentence you choose.

## 2. Meet the useful words

| Word | Arabic | British sound | Picture sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **man** | رجل | /mæn/ | **This is a man.** |
| **woman** | امرأة | /ˈwʊmən/ | **This is a woman.** |
| **child** | طفل / طفلة | /tʃaɪld/ | **This is a child.** |
| **boy** | ولد | /bɔɪ/ | **This is a boy.** |
| **girl** | بنت | /ɡɜːl/ | **This is a girl.** |
| **friend** | صديق / صديقة | /frend/ | **This is my friend.** |

### Visual meaning task

The interface shows three picture cards at a time. The learner hears **woman** and selects the matching card. Feedback uses the word in a sentence: **“Yes. This is a woman.”** It never uses a deliberately misspelled distractor.

## 3. Notice one small pattern — one person

**English explanation.** A word for one person is **singular**. You do not need to remember the grammar name today. Just notice that English often puts **a** before one person word:

> **a man**  
> **a woman**  
> **a child**  
> **a friend**

**الشرح بالعربية.** عندما نتحدث عن شخص واحد، نستخدم في الإنجليزية غالباً **a** قبل كلمة الشخص:

> **a man** = رجل واحد  
> **a woman** = امرأة واحدة  
> **a child** = طفل واحد

**Important small exception / استثناء صغير مهم:** We say **a woman**, even though the word begins with the letter **w**. The lesson does not ask the learner to master all `a/an` rules yet; it only gives useful, correct chunks.

## 4. Hear and say

> **Teacher:** Look at the picture. This is a woman.  
> **Student:** This is a woman.  
> **Teacher:** Is she your friend?  
> **Student:** Yes. She is my friend.

**Say it with a picture.** First point, then say the whole sentence:

1. **This is a man.**
2. **This is a child.**
3. **This is my friend.**

**Pronunciation focus:** `child` starts with `/tʃ/`, the first sound in **chair**. Do not add an extra vowel before it: say **child**, not “e-child”.

## 5. Use it in life

### Picture-to-sentence builder

The learner selects picture + words to build a sentence. The words appear as meaningful chunks, not as a random spelling puzzle.

| Picture | Word chunks available | Target sentence |
|---|---|---|
| A girl with a school bag | This is / a / girl | **This is a girl.** |
| A man beside a friend | He is / my / friend | **He is my friend.** |
| A woman in a photo | This is / a / woman | **This is a woman.** |

### Guided personal response

Choose one person you know, or use the picture if you prefer not to share personal information. Complete:

> **This is my ____.**

Possible answers: **friend**, **mother**, **father**. The system accepts only language already introduced or clearly labels a new optional word.

## 6. Remember and check

1. A picture shows one young person. Which sentence fits?
   - A. This is child.
   - B. **This is a child.**
   - C. This are a child.

2. Choose the word for **امرأة**.
   - A. man
   - B. **woman**
   - C. boy

3. Complete: `This is my ___.` A friend is in the picture.
   - A. happy
   - B. **friend**
   - C. are

**Feedback if needed.** “First ask: one person or many people? Here we have one person, so use the useful chunk **a + person word**.”

## Retrieval link to Lesson 5

The next speaking lesson begins with a choice: **“You meet a new person. Start with hello. Then say: My name is ____. This is my friend, ____. What do you say next?”** The learner recycles greeting, name, and people language in one spoken routine.

| Can-do outcome | **I can say a four-line introduction with a greeting, my name, a simple fact, and a polite closing.** | **أستطيع أن أقول تعريفاً من أربعة أسطر يتضمن تحية واسمي ومعلومة بسيطة ووداعاً مهذباً.** |

## 1. Start together

> **Mentor:** You have enough English for a first small conversation. You do not need to speak quickly. Speak clearly, one line at a time. Pauses are allowed.
>
> **المرشد:** لديك الآن إنجليزية كافية لأول محادثة صغيرة. لا تحتاج إلى التحدث بسرعة. تحدث بوضوح، سطراً واحداً في كل مرة. التوقف مسموح.

**Warm retrieval chain.** Without looking, choose one word from each earlier lesson:

| Lesson | Remember one item |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hello** or **Goodbye** |
| 2 | **My name is…** |
| 3 | **I am…** |
| 4 | **friend** or **woman/man/child** |

## 2. Meet the conversation frame

> **Amina:** Hello. My name is Amina.  
> **Samir:** Hi, Amina. My name is Samir.  
> **Amina:** I am a student. This is my friend, Noor.  
> **Samir:** Hello, Noor. Thank you. Goodbye.

**Arabic support / دعم بالعربية:**

> **أمينة:** مرحباً. اسمي أمينة.  
> **سمير:** أهلاً يا أمينة. اسمي سمير.  
> **أمينة:** أنا طالبة. هذه صديقتي نور.  
> **سمير:** مرحباً يا نور. شكراً. إلى اللقاء.

### Notice the conversation shape

| Part | Useful English | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Open | **Hello / Hi** | Starts the interaction. |
| 2. Introduce | **My name is…** | Gives your name. |
| 3. Add one fact | **I am a student.** | Gives a simple detail. |
| 4. Close politely | **Thank you. Goodbye.** | Ends the interaction clearly. |

## 3. Notice one small speaking pattern — English order helps the listener

**English explanation.** In a short introduction, give the listener information in a friendly order:

> **Hello.** → **My name is Amina.** → **I am a student.** → **Goodbye.**

Each line has one job. You do not need to put every word in one long sentence.

**الشرح بالعربية.** في التعريف القصير، أعطِ المستمع المعلومات بترتيب مريح:

> **Hello.** ثم **My name is…** ثم **I am…** ثم **Goodbye.**

لكل سطر وظيفة واحدة. لا تحتاج إلى وضع كل الكلمات في جملة طويلة.

## 4. Hear and say — three passes

### Pass 1: listen for meaning

Listen to the model without repeating. Tap the line that tells the speaker’s name.

### Pass 2: echo with pauses

Repeat after each line. The screen displays a breathing mark `/` where a pause helps:

> **Hello. / My name is Amina.**  
> **I am a student. / This is my friend, Noor.**

### Pass 3: substitute one true detail

Keep the frame, change one detail:

> **Hello. My name is ____. I am a student. Goodbye.**

**Pronunciation focus:** In **my name**, connect the words gently: `/maɪ neɪm/`. In **student**, the first sound is a small `/j/` after `st`: `/ˈstjuːdnt/` in British English.

## 5. Use it in life

### Role-play: meeting a classmate

The interface offers a low-pressure choice: speak aloud, type your lines, or rehearse silently first. It never scores accent. It gives feedback on whether the required communication pieces are present.

**Prompt card:** You are new in class. Meet a classmate named **Rami**.

| Turn | Rami says | Your supported response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hello. My name is Rami. | **Hi. My name is ____.** |
| 2 | Are you a student? | **Yes. I am a student.** |
| 3 | This is my friend, Dina. | **Hello, Dina.** |
| 4 | Nice to meet you. | **Thank you. Goodbye.** |

**Important accuracy note.** The phrase **Nice to meet you** is heard as optional exposure, but the learner is not assessed on it yet. The required assessment language stays within the words and structures already taught.

### Optional writing bridge

Write four short lines. Use the model, then make it yours:

> Hello.  
> My name is ____.  
> I am a student.  
> Goodbye.

**External AI feedback prompt, optional and free to the platform.**

> I am an A1 English learner whose first language is Arabic. Here is my four-line introduction: [paste your writing]. Check only these points: (1) did I use a greeting, (2) did I write “My name is…”, (3) did I use “I am…” correctly, and (4) did I close politely? Give one correction at a time. Explain in simple English and Arabic. Do not rewrite everything for me unless I ask.

## 6. Remember and check

### Conversation completion

Choose the line that makes the conversation continue naturally.

> **Rami:** Hello. My name is Rami. What is your name?  
> **You:** ______

- A. Goodbye, Rami.
- B. **Hi. My name is ____.**
- C. You are a student.

### Grammar and communication check

1. Which line gives one fact about yourself?
   - A. Hello.
   - B. My name is Layla.
   - C. **I am a student.**

2. Choose the best final line after a short first meeting.
   - A. My name is Omar.
   - B. I am a friend.
   - C. **Thank you. Goodbye.**

3. Read the exchange. Which word is missing?

   > **Noor:** Hi. My name is Noor.  
   > **Ali:** Hello, Noor. I ___ Ali.

   - A. are
   - B. **am**
   - C. friend

### Learner reflection

The learner checks one honest statement; it does not affect their score.

| Today I can… | Not yet | With the model | By myself |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| greet someone | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| say my name | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| say “I am a student” | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| close politely | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

## Retrieval link to Lesson 6

Lesson 6 uses the same introduction pattern and adds age:

> **Hello. My name is Noor. I am twenty years old.**

The learner will first retrieve the familiar lines, then add one number phrase. The new topic grows out of language they can already say.

# Cross-Lesson Review — What Has Changed Across Lessons 1–5?

| Language item | First meeting | Later use | Evidence by Lesson 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| hello / hi | Lesson 1 greeting card | Starts name exchanges and role-plays | Opens the four-line conversation. |
| thank you / goodbye | Lesson 1 closing | Closes a dialogue and supports politeness | Ends the Lesson 5 interaction appropriately. |
| my name / your name | Lesson 2 | Appears in questions and answers | Used in a real classmate-meeting exchange. |
| I am / you are | Lesson 3 | Connects a person to a simple fact | Used to give one personal detail. |
| friend / people words | Lesson 4 | Introduces a third person | Allows the learner to add another person to the conversation. |

## Review Gate for Approval

Before this style is applied to the remaining A1 lessons, review the following questions.

| Review question | Expected answer in this sample |
|---|---|
| Does each lesson have one clear A1 outcome? | Yes; each begins with a small, observable can-do statement. |
| Is Arabic support available without replacing English practice? | Yes; the learner receives Arabic explanation but still performs with English. |
| Does vocabulary return in new contexts? | Yes; greetings become name exchanges, identity sentences, people descriptions, and a full mini-conversation. |
| Is grammar explained for a near-zero learner? | Yes; it avoids unexplained terminology and makes Arabic–English differences explicit when useful. |
| Is pronunciation an action rather than a label? | Yes; each word has word-only listening and a repeat/say task. |
| Are quizzes contextual and free from misleading misspellings? | Yes; distractors are learned alternatives or meaningful grammar contrasts, not false spellings. |
| Is there a real communicative reason to use the language? | Yes; the learner meets, introduces themselves, describes a person, and closes a short exchange. |
