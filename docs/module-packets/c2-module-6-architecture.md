# C2 Module 6 Architecture — Risk, power, and adaptation

## Scope

C2 Module 6 covers Lessons 76–90. Its source inventory moves through risk communication and responsible leadership, language and public narratives, and the future of work and lifelong learning. The module asks learners to understand how public language allocates responsibility, how uncertainty should shape action, and how people can adapt without treating human lives as replaceable inputs.

| Lessons | Arc | Dominant experiences |
|---|---|---|
| 76–80 | Risk and responsible leadership | Listening, interaction, reading, speaking, checkpoint |
| 81–85 | Discourse, power, and public narratives | Reading, grammar, writing, interaction, listening |
| 86–90 | Expertise, adaptation, and lifelong learning | Reading, speaking, writing, review, assessment |

## Authoring principles

Every lesson has one primary objective and one compact bilingual retrieval check. The route changes with the objective rather than cycling through a fixed skill order. Risk lessons prioritise calibrated public explanation and decision language. Discourse lessons prioritise framing, agency, presupposition, and the difference between naming a problem and distributing responsibility for it. Adaptation lessons prioritise synthesis, transfer, and humane descriptions of expertise.

Reading is used for frame comparison, implication, and institutional voice. Listening is audio-first, with transcripts hidden until the first attempt. Writing prompts are designed for external-AI feedback and require a defined audience, visible evidence, and a revision condition. Speaking and interaction appear when the learner must answer a challenge, negotiate a public message, or explain a transition to another person.

## Progressive disclosure

The first view always answers what the experience is, what the learner should do, what matters, and what comes next. C2 support remains optional and staged: vocabulary support, Arabic explanation, worked examples, extended rationale, and transcripts are not all shown at once. The module uses light review and checkpoint lessons to prevent every experience from becoming a deep reading or writing task.

## Assessment intent

Lesson 80 is a checkpoint on risk, uncertainty, and accountable leadership. Lesson 90 is the module transfer assessment. The learner receives a short public narrative and an adaptation or work-transition scenario, then produces a briefing, dialogue, or recommendation for a specified audience. Strong performance keeps uncertainty visible, identifies who gains and who bears risk, notices how labels frame responsibility, and proposes an adaptation that can be evaluated without reducing people to productivity metrics.

## Quality gates

The implementation must include one authored activity and one adaptive experience for every lesson in the range, at least seven activity kinds, at least four reading routes with checks, at least three writing routes, at least two transcript-supported listening routes, explicit Arabic retrieval on every activity, a checkpoint at Lesson 80, and an assessment-semantic final lesson. It must pass focused C2 tests, the complete Vitest suite, TypeScript, production build, curriculum audit, and rearchitecture audit.

## Decision

Module 6 is approved as a **risk-and-adaptation studio**. It builds directly on Module 5's attention to framing and revision while expanding the learner's ability to communicate responsibility, uncertainty, power, and humane change.

## References
