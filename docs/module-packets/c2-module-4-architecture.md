# C2 Module 4 Architecture

## Learning arc

C2 Module 4 (Lessons 46–60) develops **public interpretation under pressure**. The learner moves from explaining scientific uncertainty and cultural belonging to communicating risk, analysing public framing, designing adaptive learning, adjudicating competing rights, and synthesising evidence into a defensible public position. The recurring question is not simply “What does this mean?” but “Whose interests, assumptions, and consequences become visible when I choose this wording?”

The module therefore treats advanced English as **ethical mediation**: the learner must preserve distinctions, qualify claims, represent people fairly, and adjust register without erasing disagreement. The final lessons require the learner to combine source evaluation, rhetorical control, audience awareness, and revision into a public-facing recommendation.

| Lesson range | Dominant movement | Learner-facing question |
|---|---|---|
| 46–48 | Uncertainty, belonging, and mediation | How can I explain complex evidence or lived experience without reducing it? |
| 49–51 | Risk, framing, and institutional language | How can I make responsibility and uncertainty visible under pressure? |
| 52–54 | Work, rights, and public fairness | How can I compare values and propose proportionate action? |
| 55–57 | Interpretation, science, and evidence transfer | How can I defend a reading while keeping its limits explicit? |
| 58–60 | Public synthesis and judgement | How can I turn competing sources into a credible, audience-aware position? |

## Archetype balance

The curriculum architect selects the dominant experience from the objective and source task rather than rotating mechanically. Module 4 includes mediation, notice-and-reframe, reading studio, listening briefing, interaction, formal writing, speaking rehearsal, real-world decision, review, and transfer assessment routes. At least two lessons deliberately foreground source comparison, two use audio-first disclosure, two require spoken or interactive negotiation, and the final lessons privilege synthesis over new lexical load.

## Skill cadence

Reading appears throughout the module because C2 learners must work from heterogeneous sources, but it alternates between close annotation, comparison, and public mediation. Listening is attached to authentic uncertainty and crisis communication, with transcripts hidden until a first listening attempt. Writing appears in compact formal genres before the final synthesis. Speaking and interaction are used when the objective is stance negotiation, risk briefing, or rights-based deliberation. Retrieval is present in every lesson and links back to C1 evidence, perspective, consequence, and evaluation without forcing unnatural vocabulary.

## Retrieval and progressive disclosure

Every lesson opens with a short bilingual retrieval check. The check asks the learner to recover a prior concept, distinguish two near-synonyms, complete a context-sensitive phrase, or restate a claim with a different degree of certainty. Arabic is used for orientation and contrast, while the target response remains English. Explanatory notes, transcripts, alternative interpretations, and model answers are disclosed after an attempt rather than placed in the first view.

## Assessment intent

Lesson 50 provides a small checkpoint on uncertainty and framing. Lesson 60 is the module transfer assessment: the learner receives a compact source pack containing a technical claim, a lived account, and an institutional response, then produces a public briefing or recommendation. The assessment checks evidence fidelity, qualification, agency, register, cohesion, and ethical representation. External prompts may be copied to a learner-selected AI for feedback; no per-request AI grading is required inside the application.

## Quality gates

The implementation must compile against the shared C2 activity contract, expose one authored activity plan for Lessons 46–60, provide an adaptive experience for every lesson, include bilingual retrieval evidence in every authored route, preserve transcript disclosure on listening routes, and pass TypeScript, production build, curriculum audit, rearchitecture audit, focused regressions, and the full Vitest suite before checkpointing.
