# B2 Module 3 — Research and Curriculum-Architecture Packet

> **Status:** Architecture approved for authoring; learner-facing activities must not be written until this packet is preserved in the content handoff.

## 1. Module purpose

This fifteen-lesson B2 module develops the learner from stating a supported opinion to **evaluating public choices under uncertainty**. Its coherent journey is: access and representation → evidence and risk → trust and negotiation → media, identity, and belonging → public argument, ethical technology, evaluative review, and a consequence-aware proposal. Learners should use formal, nuanced English to make fairer, more accountable recommendations—not merely produce persuasive language.

The authoritative draft calls for public-space design, participation, reform, risk, credibility, negotiation, media influence, inequality, translation and identity, belonging, counterargument, automation, criticism, disagreement, and policy consequences. The rearchitecture preserves that scope while preventing an identical “read, explain, write, quiz” route from appearing in every lesson.

## 2. Research boundaries and learner safeguards

| Area | Design decision | Learner safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Public policy and inequality | Use invented, local-scale scenarios rather than asking learners to disclose political allegiance or personal hardship. | Evidence must be bounded; no activity treats a viewpoint as the only reasonable answer. |
| Disability, access, and inclusion | Use respectful people-first wording where appropriate, and focus on design barriers rather than deficits. | Include accessibility and Arabic clarification support without reducing the learner to a passive recipient. |
| Health, safety, and risk | Teach calibrated language, not personal health advice or diagnosis. | State uncertainty plainly and avoid medical claims. |
| Identity and culture | Invite comparison of texts and representations, not essentialising claims about groups. | Require hedges such as *may*, *tends to*, *in this source*, and *some participants*. |
| Technology and bias | Make responsibility, assumptions, and affected groups visible. | Do not present automation as neutral or inevitable. |

## 3. Architectural decisions

The dominant experience is chosen by the lesson’s learning job. **Stages are not layouts**: a reading route might omit an explicit grammar explanation, while a speaking route may move from an encounter straight to supported use. The module uses a mix of light, normal, and deep lessons; the two deep capstone tasks are deliberately separated by several lower-load lessons.

## 4. Required variation checks

The authored corpus must meet the following **module-level** constraints before pedagogical review.

| Requirement | Target | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Density | 3 light, 7 normal, 5 deep lessons (±1 only with written rationale) | Marking every lesson deep to make the module appear substantial. |
| Activity sequence | No adjacent lessons may share the same ordered activity-kind signature. | Changing vocabulary only while retaining the same route. |
| Modality | Reading: 3–4; writing: 2–3; speaking/interaction: 4–6; grammar/notice: 3–4; assessment: 1. | Forcing reading, writing, speaking, and listening into every lesson. |
| Retrieval | At least six lessons retrieve B2 Module 1–2 or relevant B1 content naturally; Lesson 41 and 45 require cumulative retrieval. | Repeating old words only because a retrieval quota is due. |
| Arabic support | Provide precise Arabic clarification at the first encounter of high-stakes abstraction; reduce it during transfer. | Duplicating every English sentence in Arabic. |
| Learner clarity | Every first view answers: **What is this? What do I do? What matters? What’s next?** | Hiding the task inside a long mentor paragraph. |

## 6. Assessment map

| Assessment point | Evidence sampled | Feedback focus | Non-negotiable fairness rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson 33 reading evidence map | Claims, sources, warrants, counterclaims | Distinguish detail from implication and source from conclusion. | Accept more than one defensible interpretation if it is text-grounded. |
| Lesson 41 argument integration | Reasoning, evidence selection, fair rebuttal | Strength of reasoning and calibration—not strength of political position. | Use fictional local proposals; never reward a particular policy preference. |
| Lesson 43 evaluative review | Criteria, appraisal language, revision | Specific criteria and proportionate language. | Assess writing choices, not the learner’s consumer preferences. |
| Lesson 45 policy proposal | Recommendation, qualification, projected outcomes, review plan | Connection between evidence, trade-off, and proposed action. | The proposed solution is not scored for ideology; its reasoning is. |

## 7. Content-creator guardrails

1. Do not reuse the same mentor opening, dialogue structure, question format, or activity sequence merely because the topic changes.
2. Do not fabricate quotations, statistics, testimonials, or authorities. Use clearly fictional local scenarios and label examples as examples.
3. Do not make the learner state a political, religious, medical, or personal identity to complete a task.
4. Use British English as the default spelling and pronunciation reference.
5. Use only valid lesson-progression stages and progressive supports from the shared type contract.
6. Prefer a meaningful omission over a redundant activity: a lesson does not need all skills to be valuable.
