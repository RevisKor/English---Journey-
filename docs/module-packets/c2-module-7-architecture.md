# C2 Module 7 Architecture — Rights, Design, and Public Futures

## Scope

C2 Module 7 covers Lessons 91–105. It follows Module 6's work on risk, discourse power, and humane adaptation by asking a harder question: **when change is presented as inevitable, whose rights, access, and future are being designed into the decision?** The module moves from lifelong learning and expertise into competing rights, then into the ethics of global-city design.

| Lessons | Arc | Dominant experiences |
|---|---|---|
| 91–95 | Expertise, transition, and public learning | Reading, interaction, listening, writing, checkpoint |
| 96–100 | Law, fairness, and competing rights | Grammar, reading, speaking, interaction, checkpoint |
| 101–105 | Cities, access, and ethical design | Listening, reading, writing, review, transfer assessment |

## Primary objective

The learner will mediate complex public decisions in which evidence, rights, access, and long-term consequences compete. The module is not legal training or urban-planning training. It is advanced English practice in qualification, comparison, fairness, institutional voice, audience awareness, and defensible recommendation.

## Authoring principles

Every lesson has one dominant objective and one compact bilingual retrieval check. Lesson experiences are selected by the work the learner must perform, not by a fixed skill rotation. The first arc treats expertise and transition as questions of access, recognition, and learning design. The second arc introduces competing rights and tests proportionality, jurisdiction, precedent, remedy, and equitable treatment. The final arc turns from abstract fairness to designed space: infrastructure, accessibility, density, displacement, liveability, and retrofitting.

Reading tasks ask learners to compare claims, trace definitions, identify legal or design assumptions, and infer who is included or excluded. Listening is audio-first with transcript support hidden until the first attempt. Interaction and speaking require a learner to question a premise, explain a trade-off, or preserve disagreement without collapsing it into false balance. Writing tasks are designed for copyable external-AI feedback and require a defined audience, source fidelity, qualification, and a revision trigger.

## Progressive disclosure

Each experience answers four first-view questions: what this is, what to do, what matters, and what comes next. C2 support is staged: vocabulary support, Arabic explanation, worked examples, extended rationale, and transcripts appear only when useful. Light lessons are intentionally concise; deep lessons carry the densest source comparison or writing work. The module does not force every stage into every lesson.

## Retrieval and cadence

Retrieval connects Module 6 ideas—risk distribution, agency, responsibility, expertise, and revisability—to the new work only where the connection is natural. Reading appears in Lessons 91, 93, 96, 98, 102, and 104. Writing appears in Lessons 94, 99, and 103. Listening appears in Lessons 93, 97, and 101. Lesson 95 is a checkpoint on expertise and transition. Lesson 100 is a checkpoint on rights and proportionality. Lesson 105 is a transfer assessment requiring an evidence-faithful recommendation about a contested design or policy decision.

## Assessment intent

The final task gives the learner a compact source pack: a policy extract about a right, a resident or worker account, and a design or access commentary. The learner must identify the competing interests, distinguish equal treatment from equitable treatment, make a proportionate recommendation, preserve uncertainty, and specify what evidence would trigger revision. Strong performance demonstrates precision without legal overclaiming, accessible explanation without flattening disagreement, and visible attention to people usually omitted from a design brief.

## Quality gates

The implementation must include one authored activity and one adaptive experience for every lesson in the range, at least seven activity kinds, at least five reading routes with checks, at least three writing routes, at least three transcript-supported listening routes, explicit Arabic retrieval on every activity, checkpoints at Lessons 95 and 100, and an assessment-semantic final lesson. It must pass focused C2 tests, the complete Vitest suite, TypeScript, production build, curriculum audit, and rearchitecture audit.

## Decision

Module 7 is approved as a **rights-and-design mediation studio**. It extends the learner's C2 control from communicating risk and power to evaluating fairness and translating contested public decisions into precise, accessible, and revisable recommendations.
