# C1 Module 3 Architecture — Evidence, Policy, and Public Meaning

## Module purpose

C1 Module 3 develops the learner’s ability to interpret contested public information, distinguish evidence from testimony and institutional framing, and convert that interpretation into concise, defensible proposals. The module treats advanced English as a tool for judgment: learners must qualify claims, acknowledge uncertainty, compare sources, and communicate recommendations to a defined public audience.

The module is deliberately **compact and objective-led**. Each of Lessons 33–48 has one primary communicative objective and one short bilingual retrieval check. The activity routes vary across source reading, listening, noticing, speaking, interaction, formal writing, review, and assessment; they are not arranged as a mechanical skill rotation.

## Source-to-journey map

| Lessons | Learner journey | Dominant archetype | Primary objective | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 33–34 | Establishing a claim and testing its scope | Notice → Reading | Separate a source’s central claim from its limits and implied assumptions. | Annotated source extract and claim map |
| 35–36 | From data to qualified interpretation | Listening → Grammar/Notice | Use hedging, evidential verbs, and calibrated modality to avoid overstating a pattern. | Audio briefing with hidden transcript; sentence revision |
| 37–38 | Voices, interests, and institutional framing | Interaction → Reading | Compare first-hand testimony with an institutional account without collapsing their different evidential roles. | Paired-source comparison and oral mediation |
| 39–40 | Causal explanations in public language | Grammar → Writing | Express causal relationships through precise abstract noun phrases and controlled nominalisation. | Transformation workshop and concise explanatory paragraph |
| 41–42 | Counterfactual responsibility | Speaking → Listening | Evaluate an intervention through counterfactual and ethical conditional reasoning. | Audio hearing and structured response |
| 43–44 | Evidence-aware recommendation | Reading → Writing | Synthesize two sources into a formal recommendation with explicit attribution and a practical safeguard. | Policy extract, community statement, proposal draft |
| 45 | Review under pressure | Review | Retrieve the module’s claim, stance, source, and proposal language in a new scenario. | Timed retrieval circuit |
| 46 | Public issue, privately felt | Integration | Reconcile policy evidence and lived experience in a balanced formal proposal. | Council-committee brief |
| 47 | The ethics of convenience | Assessment | Defend a conditional position on consent and responsibility using multiple sources. | Data-protection submission |
| 48 | Beyond a single story | Assessment | Interpret contested representation, challenge a prevailing narrative respectfully, and propose concrete change. | Arts-council synthesis brief |

## Cadence controls

Reading appears frequently, generally every one or two lessons, because C1 learners must sustain source interpretation and compare rhetorical framing. Writing recurs every two to three lessons but remains focused: short analytical paragraphs precede the longer proposal tasks. Listening is audio-first in Lessons 35, 42, and 47, with transcripts disclosed only after an initial listening attempt. Speaking and interaction are distributed around source mediation and public-hearing tasks rather than appended as generic practice. Grammar is embedded where it serves a discourse problem—hedging, nominalisation, and conditionals—and is not placed in consecutive grammar-heavy lessons without a communicative reason.

| Control | Module 3 decision |
|---|---|
| Primary objective | Exactly one per authored lesson, with an Arabic rendering. |
| Retrieval | Every lesson ends with a short English/Arabic retrieval record tied to the target decision or language move. |
| Reading | Frequent, source-aware, and progressively multi-source; no passage is included merely to fill a skill slot. |
| Writing | Short evidence paragraphs first; formal proposal and synthesis tasks later. |
| Listening | Audio-first routes disclose transcripts progressively and preserve replay controls. |
| Speaking/interaction | Mediating, qualifying, and defending interpretations for a real audience. |
| Review | Lesson 45 is the dedicated module retrieval bridge before the cumulative assessments. |
| Assessment | Lessons 46–48 provide increasing independence, source complexity, audience sensitivity, and synthesis. |

## Progression stages

The sequence moves through **orientation, encounter, notice, supported practice, meaningful use, retrieval, evidence, and next bridge** without forcing every stage into every lesson. Early lessons foreground source orientation and claim boundaries. Middle lessons move into calibrated language, comparison, and causal explanation. The final lessons require independent synthesis, respectful disagreement, and concrete recommendation. The learner should finish with a usable public-facing proposal, not a list of isolated grammar rules.

## Arabic-speaker support and progressive disclosure

Arabic support appears in titles, objectives, retrieval checks, and mentor transitions. Technical terms are introduced through plain-language definitions and immediately paired with an example. Potentially dense material is disclosed in layers: first the task and source, then optional annotation, then language notes, then the transcript or model comparison. The UI should not display every source note, translation, and assessment criterion at once. Audio transcripts remain hidden by default; evidence annotations and counterargument prompts appear when the learner requests them.

## Assessment arc

Lessons 33–44 build the component skills needed for source-aware judgment. Lesson 45 retrieves those moves in a fresh context. Lessons 46–48 form a three-step cumulative assessment arc: reconcile lived experience with policy evidence; evaluate an ethical technology decision under conditions; and challenge a contested public narrative through respectful synthesis. Success requires accurate attribution, calibrated claims, audience-sensitive register, clear causal or conditional logic, and a practical recommendation. External AI grading remains prompt-based and transparent; no per-request in-site model call is required.

## Architecture acceptance criteria

The module is ready for integration only when all sixteen lessons have an explicit bilingual retrieval check, no lesson bundles unrelated primary objectives, at least three audio-first or listening-supported routes expose transcripts progressively, reading and writing cadence remains within the C1 range, activity archetypes are visibly varied, and the final assessments require transfer to a new context rather than recall of the source scenarios.
