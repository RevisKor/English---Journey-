# C2 Module 2 Architecture

## Module identity

**Title:** Diplomacy, mediation, and public narratives

**Arabic:** الدبلوماسية والوساطة والسرديات العامة

**Lessons:** 16–30

**Learning arc:** The module begins with difficult disagreement and repair, then develops the learner’s ability to translate expertise, persuade ethically, synthesize evidence, construct qualified arguments, discuss technology and ecological thresholds, compare memory narratives, evaluate markets and social value, rewrite institutional language, interpret art, explain scientific uncertainty, discuss migration and belonging, communicate crisis risk, and analyse how public discourse distributes power. The central C2 move is from having a sophisticated position to making that position usable, accountable, and intelligible to different audiences.

## Authoring principles

Each lesson has one dominant objective and one short bilingual retrieval check. Activities are compact but intellectually demanding: a lesson may foreground mediation, reading, listening, interaction, speaking, writing, grammar, review, or assessment without mechanically containing every skill. Prior C1 evidence, qualification, perspective, responsibility, and uncertainty are retrieved only where they serve the current task.

The module deliberately varies lesson archetypes. The sequence includes a mediation workshop, source-comparison reading, ethical persuasion interaction, synthesis assessment, precision grammar, technology debate, ecological explanation, narrative comparison, value-judgement review, institutional rewriting, interpretive reading, audio-first science explanation, intercultural negotiation, crisis briefing, discourse analysis, and a final transfer route. The order is an intentional arc rather than a fixed reading–writing–speaking–listening rotation.

## Skill cadence and disclosure

Reading appears throughout the module through source comparison, institutional language, art interpretation, science communication, and discourse analysis. Writing recurs through mediated explanations, transparent persuasion, argument qualification, policy language, and public risk communication. Listening is audio-first in lessons where tone, uncertainty, or crisis pacing matters; transcripts are hidden until the learner has attempted the listening target. Interaction and speaking are used when the objective is negotiation, ethical choice, audience adaptation, or credible public delivery.

| Design area | Module 2 decision |
| --- | --- |
| Retrieval | Every lesson contains English and Arabic prompts with observable evidence |
| Reading | Source-aware, often multi-perspective, with claims, limits, and interpretation checks |
| Writing | Audience-specific mediation, qualification, institutional rewriting, and public briefing |
| Listening | Progressive transcript disclosure; first listen targets stance, uncertainty, or repair |
| Speaking | Negotiation, explanation, crisis communication, and public narrative delivery |
| Interaction | Purposeful turns represent interests, challenge assumptions, or repair disagreement |
| Visual support | Used sparingly for discourse maps, evidence relations, or public-language framing |
| Review | Lessons 19, 24, and 30 consolidate prior distinctions without becoming vocabulary dumps |
| Assessment | Lesson 30 requires discourse analysis plus a responsible public rewrite |

## Assessment intent

The final lesson is a transfer assessment. The learner analyses a public narrative, identifies framing choices and affected groups, distinguishes evidence from value judgement, and rewrites the communication for a defined audience while preserving accuracy and accountability. The assessment should reward precision, calibrated claims, ethical awareness, register control, and reader effort rather than ornamental complexity.

All writing remains compatible with the project’s external-AI prompt model. The website supplies the task, target language, audience, and a structured grading request; the learner chooses an external tool and remains responsible for reviewing the feedback. No per-request AI dependency is introduced into the authored content.
