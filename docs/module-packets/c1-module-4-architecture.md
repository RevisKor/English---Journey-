# C1 Module 4 Architecture — Evidence, Uncertainty, and Public Decisions

## Module purpose

C1 Module 4 turns advanced source interpretation into responsible public decision-making. The authoritative draft places the learner in municipal and infrastructure contexts where sources disagree, evidence is incomplete, and a useful recommendation must be both decisive and properly qualified. The module therefore prioritises **epistemic control**: learners distinguish claims from evidence, compare methods, attribute positions, calibrate certainty, identify bias and missing data, and convert synthesis into an audience-sensitive proposal.

The Arabic support is deliberately conceptual rather than word-for-word. It clarifies the difference between *claim*, *evidence*, *inference*, *limitation*, and *recommendation* so Arabic-speaking learners can transfer the reasoning into English without reducing advanced work to vocabulary memorisation.

## Operating constraints

| Control | Module 4 decision |
|---|---|
| Primary objective | One dominant evidence or communication move per lesson; no lesson is an all-skills bundle. |
| Retrieval | Every lesson ends with one short bilingual retrieval/check record that asks the learner to reuse the current move in a fresh context. |
| Reading | Frequent source work, normally every 1–2 lessons, with paired or contrasting sources rather than isolated comprehension. |
| Writing | Four substantial writing moments across the module, including a final executive recommendation; drafts require attribution, stance, cohesion, and audience control. |
| Listening | Audio-first source briefings and spoken committee evidence appear frequently; transcripts remain disclosed progressively rather than being the first view. |
| Speaking | Learners rehearse calibrated claims, clarification, challenge, and recommendation language in short turns rather than performing a generic discussion every time. |
| Interaction / real world | Committee roles, evidence conferences, source audits, and decision briefings appear where they serve the objective. |
| Grammar / lexis | Grammar is embedded in stance, attribution, hedging, nominalisation, concessive framing, and reporting rather than isolated as consecutive grammar lessons. |
| Assessment | Lessons 54, 59, and 64 function as local evidence checkpoints; Lesson 64 is the module assessment and requires transfer to a new policy context. |
| Density | Light lessons isolate a reasoning move; normal lessons combine one source task with one controlled production task; deep lessons reserve space for synthesis or assessed writing. |

## Cadence rationale

The sequence is not a fixed reading–writing–speaking rotation. Lessons 49–54 establish the analytical vocabulary and culminate in the first checkpoint. Lessons 55–59 deepen methodological comparison and move into a short synthesis brief. Lessons 60–64 increase independence: the learner handles provenance, stakeholder disagreement, formal recommendation, oral defence, and transfer assessment. Listening appears in Lessons 52, 56, and 61 because C1 learners must process evidence in real time; transcripts are support, not the task itself. Writing appears in Lessons 57, 59, and 62, with the final assessment adding an audience-sensitive proposal rather than simply increasing word count.

## Assessment design

The module assessment does not reward a confident opinion alone. It samples five separable capabilities: accurate claim extraction, method-sensitive evidence appraisal, responsible attribution, calibrated recommendation language, and transfer to a new context. A learner may receive partial credit for a defensible decision with imperfect language, but cannot pass the evidence criterion by citing sources without explaining their relevance or limits. Arabic retrieval prompts support metacognition while the assessed proposal remains English-first.

## Progressive disclosure and visual intent

Every first view answers **What is this? What do I do? What matters? What comes next?** Source cards disclose title, provenance, and task before showing explanatory notes. Audio lessons show the listening purpose and playback controls first; transcript, translation support, and vocabulary notes remain behind an explicit reveal. Claim, evidence, limitation, attribution, retrieval, and assessment markers use the shared semantic labels and icons, never colour alone. Dense dossiers are chunked into source panels and margin-note actions so the interface remains calm even when the reasoning is advanced.

## Completion standard

Module 4 is complete only when all sixteen records are bilingual, compact, and objective-led; every lesson has an explicit retrieval/check; reading, writing, listening, speaking, and interaction meet the C1 cadence without mechanical alternation; the assessment route is distinct from ordinary practice; the activity corpus and experience map compile against the shared contracts; the live C1 pipeline selects the authored records for Lessons 49–64; and the learner workspace preserves the first un-authored fallback after Lesson 64.
