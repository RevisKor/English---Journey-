# C2 Module 1 Architecture — Precision, Mediation, and Independent Judgement

## Scope and learning arc

C2 Module 1 covers Lessons 1–15 of the 180-lesson C2 course. Its purpose is not to introduce a larger word list mechanically; it establishes the C2 studio habit of making deliberate choices about **evidence, audience, register, uncertainty, framing, and ethical consequence**. The learner moves from constructing a qualified argument to mediating scientific and legal complexity, then toward public communication, cross-cultural negotiation, and a final synthesis in which language is treated as an instrument of judgement.

| Arc | Lessons | Learner movement |
|---|---:|---|
| Evidence and systems | 1–5 | Analyse premises, technology, ecology, memory, labour, and the difference between measurement and value. |
| Public language and interpretation | 6–10 | Make institutional language accountable, defend interpretations without closing ambiguity, communicate science, and discuss belonging without reduction. |
| Risk, power, and adaptation | 11–15 | Lead through uncertainty, expose framing, plan for changing work, weigh competing rights, and mediate urban-design trade-offs. |

The generated C2 source records remain the authoritative lexical and grammar reference. The authored layer adds a compact learner journey around each record: one dominant communicative objective, one explicit retrieval check, a bilingual orientation, and a carefully selected activity route. It deliberately does not force every lesson to contain reading, writing, listening, speaking, grammar, and assessment at once.

## Authoring decisions

At C2, the main progression is from **accurate expression** to **responsible control of effect**. Vocabulary is therefore organised around lexical networks, collocation, register, and stance. Grammar is taught as a rhetorical choice: inversion can foreground, nominalisation can compress or obscure, reporting verbs can position a source, and a passive can either manage focus or conceal responsibility. Arabic support is available as a precision scaffold, not as a replacement for sustained English processing.

The first-view orientation of every lesson answers four questions in English and Arabic: what the experience is, what the learner should do, what matters in the decision, and what comes next. Extended explanations, transcripts, alternative wording, and rationale are progressively disclosed. Listening work is audio-first in intent; the transcript remains hidden until the learner has made an initial interpretation. Writing tasks use external-AI prompts for optional feedback and do not create per-request model costs inside the platform.

## Archetype and skill balance

The module uses a non-rotational balance. Reading and mediation appear frequently because C2 learners must interpret demanding source material; writing appears in strategic intervals as a product of prior analysis; interaction and speaking are used where judgement must be negotiated; listening is used for source-positioning, crisis communication, and diplomatic repair. Review and assessment are embedded rather than appended mechanically.

| Archetype family | Planned emphasis | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Reading / mediation | High | C2 work depends on comparing sources, detecting omissions, and preserving qualification while changing audience. |
| Grammar / notice | Moderate | Form is selected for rhetorical effect and genre control, not isolated rule coverage. |
| Interaction / real-world | Moderate | Learners must make disagreement, consultation, and institutional choices usable. |
| Listening / speaking | Frequent but targeted | Advanced comprehension includes stance, implication, reassurance, and pragmatic pressure. |
| Writing | Frequent but spaced | Products include evidence notes, policy memos, mediation summaries, case notes, and public briefings. |
| Review / assessment | Embedded and cumulative | Retrieval must prove transfer across domains rather than recognition of isolated terms. |

No lesson is selected simply to satisfy a sequence. The authored experience map records why its dominant archetype is appropriate and which stages are intentionally omitted.

## Retrieval design

Every lesson contains a short bilingual retrieval check. The check reaches backward to C1-level ideas such as evidence, perspective, consequence, and evaluation, then asks the learner to use that idea in the current C2 context. Expected evidence is explicit so the learner can self-check before moving on. Retrieval is deliberately brief: it is a bridge into the new judgement task, not a second lesson.

Across the module, retrieval changes form: qualify a claim, identify a hidden agent, distinguish correlation from causation, reconstruct a concession, compare a public label with the people it names, or state the condition under which a recommendation would change. This keeps retrieval meaningful and avoids a universal quiz pattern.

## Assessment intent

Lesson-level activities culminate in a compact transfer route rather than a content dump. The learner should be able to mediate a difficult source for a defined audience, write a qualified recommendation, negotiate disagreement, and explain what remains uncertain. The Module 1 assessment samples these operations across evidence, register, framing, ethics, and audience adaptation. Success is judged by precision, proportion, source fidelity, coherence, and control of implication—not by using every target word.

## Quality gates

The module is ready only when the authored corpus and experience map compile against the shared contracts; every lesson has bilingual retrieval and first-view orientation; reading, writing, listening, speaking, interaction, review, and assessment routes appear with a defensible C2 cadence; transcript disclosure is explicit for listening; the C2 pipeline gives authored records precedence; and the full TypeScript, build, curriculum-audit, rearchitecture-audit, and Vitest gates pass. A dedicated quality-review packet will record pedagogical, visual/disclosure, assessment, and engineering evidence after implementation.
