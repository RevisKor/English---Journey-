# B2 Module 7 Architecture Packet

## Scope and governing intent

B2 Module 7 covers Lessons 91–105. The authoritative draft names the arc **Appraisal, disagreement, proposals, pressure, systems, and the case for change**. Its purpose is not to repeat a fixed reading–writing–speaking sequence. Instead, each lesson has one dominant learner job, a short retrieval/check, and only the supporting language work needed to make that job meaningful.

The module sits after B2 Module 6’s media, digital-life, and source-awareness work. It therefore moves from evaluating information and decisions toward defending positions, reformulating disagreement, proposing consequences, and making a qualified case for change. The module should feel more demanding than B2 Modules 1–6 through greater discourse control, register awareness, cohesion, and counterfactual reasoning—not through packing every skill into every lesson.

## Authoring controls

| Control | Module 7 decision |
|---|---|
| Primary objective | Exactly one clear objective per lesson, visible in English and Arabic. |
| Retrieval | Every lesson includes one short bilingual retrieval/check that revisits a relevant prior idea, form, or lexical distinction. It is evidence of recall, not a second lesson. |
| Density | Use light, normal, and deep lessons. Deep lessons may include a substantial reading or writing task, but still keep one dominant purpose. |
| Reading cadence | B2 reading is frequent, approximately every 1–2 lessons, but reading is not forced into every activity route. |
| Writing cadence | B2 writing appears approximately every 3–4 lessons, with revision or audience control where appropriate. |
| Listening and speaking | Include distinct listening and speaking evidence in the module; do not count one as the other. Use audio-first disclosure with optional transcript support. |
| Interaction/real world | Use interaction or real-world tasks where the lesson objective requires negotiation, challenge, proposal, or public explanation. |
| Grammar | Space grammar-focused lessons between discourse and task lessons. Grammar is not the dominant focus in consecutive lessons unless the progression requires it. |
| Review and assessment | Embed retrieval continuously, use a dedicated review around the module’s 8–12 lesson rhythm, and end Lesson 105 with a module assessment. Lessons 100 and 105 are checkpoint-sensitive points in the wider B2 assessment architecture. |
| Disclosure | Reveal rich source, grammar, and evidence detail progressively. Do not make the learner face the full data model at once. |

## Skill-balance interpretation

The sequence intentionally uses reading at Lessons 91, 95, 101, and within the assessment at 105; writing at Lessons 98, 103, and the assessment; listening distinctly at Lesson 97; and speaking at Lessons 96 and 104. Interaction and real-world work appear at Lessons 92, 93, 99, and 102. This is a balancing window, not a rotation. Reading and writing are concentrated where source interpretation and public reasoning require them, while short retrieval checks keep the rest of the module connected without inflating lesson scope.

The module’s grammar focus is distributed across Lessons 94, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, and 104, but grammar is subordinate to discourse purpose except in the deliberately light Lesson 94. Lesson 100 is a compact review rather than another new grammar lecture. Lesson 105 is the end-of-module assessment and must evaluate transfer, not merely recognition of forms.

## Arabic-speaker and B2 support decisions

Arabic explanations should clarify appraisal strength, concession, hedging, passive reporting, nominalisation, cleft emphasis, and conditional reasoning without replacing English engagement. Common transfer risks include treating all reporting verbs as equally strong, using direct Arabic-style assertion where English expects qualification, overusing universal claims, and omitting the explicit subject or agent in formal passive reporting. Supports should be progressive: a brief Arabic-help option or worked example first, followed by English-first evidence and an optional transcript or explanation.

## Handoff acceptance criteria

The authoring agent must create fifteen explicit bilingual activity records for Lessons 91–105. Each record must expose one objective, one compact retrieval/check, a valid archetype and density, valid progression stages, and a payload appropriate to its dominant experience. No lesson may receive a universal activity bundle merely to increase apparent fullness. The pedagogical reviewer must verify the skill window, the visual reviewer must verify semantic labels and progressive disclosure, and the assessment reviewer must verify that Lesson 105 measures evidence-led transfer and refutation.
