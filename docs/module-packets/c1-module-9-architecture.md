## Module purpose

C1 Module 9 advances the learner from evaluating public claims to deciding what should count as progress, who bears the cost of a decision, and how disagreement can be organised responsibly. The module is not a mechanical repetition of Modules 7–8: it moves from source-aware interpretation toward **public metrics, trade-offs, deliberation, historical memory, and accountable proposals**.

The sixteen-lesson arc is:

> indicators of progress → policy trade-offs → deliberative design → historical memory → culture in motion → limits of expertise → consequential review → readable complexity → proposal under pressure → epistemic humility → crowded debate → synthesis with integrity → implicit framing → methodological scrutiny → uncertainty language → conflicting sources.

Every lesson has one primary objective and a short bilingual retrieval check. Activities remain compact and archetype-led. The architecture deliberately returns to evidence handling while changing the learner's audience, source set, and consequence. Reading is frequent because the module's aims depend on comparing sources; writing and interaction appear when they convert analysis into responsible action.

## Skill and retrieval cadence

Reading is used in Lessons 129, 130, 132, 133, 138, 141, and 142, with source reading embedded again in the final assessment. Listening is used in Lessons 135 and 139, with transcript disclosure after an initial attempt. Writing is used in Lessons 136 and 140, with formal proposal writing embedded in Lessons 129, 130, 137, 143, and 144. Speaking and interaction are concentrated in Lessons 131, 137, and 139. Lesson 129 starts the module with a progress-metric interpretation task; Lesson 140 serves as the ten-lesson synthesis checkpoint; Lesson 144 is the module assessment.

Retrieval checks target transferable evidence moves rather than isolated vocabulary recall. Learners retrieve metric versus lived outcome, benefit versus distributional cost, consultation versus token participation, precedent versus proof, expertise versus authority, confidence versus certainty, and framing versus omission. Each check includes English and Arabic prompts plus bilingual expected evidence.

## Progressive disclosure and external-AI policy

Listening transcripts remain hidden by default and appear only after the learner has made an initial listening attempt. Reading support is progressively revealed through word-level help, Arabic explanation, and worked examples. Writing activities provide copyable bilingual prompts for external AI feedback; the website does not invoke a paid model per request. Learners choose their external tool and decide what text they are willing to share.

## Assessment architecture

Lesson 144 presents a fresh dossier about a public development programme whose headline indicators conflict with distributional evidence, historical testimony, and a methodological critique. The learner must attribute at least three sources, distinguish what each can establish, identify a likely gap or bias, acknowledge uncertainty, explain who bears a consequence, and write a 360–440-word formal proposal with a concrete review trigger. The assessment measures source comparison, calibrated language, ethical distributional reasoning, and actionable synthesis rather than memorised wording.

## Handoff requirements

The authored corpus must provide one compact activity per lesson, bilingual learner-facing fields, explicit retrieval, reading checks where appropriate, external-AI writing support, listening lines with transcript disclosure, interaction turns with purposes, and a distinct assessment record for Lesson 144. The adaptive experience map must provide all four first-view orientation fields and use at least eight archetypes across the module. The C1 pipeline must give Module 9 precedence over earlier generated or authored fallbacks, and the learner-workspace fallback must advance to Lesson 145 after validation.
