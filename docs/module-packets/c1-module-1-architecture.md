# C1 Module 1 Architecture — Reading Between the Lines

## Module purpose

C1 Module 1 moves the learner from B2 evidence handling into **source-aware interpretation and responsible synthesis**. The module is not a sequence of identical reading-and-writing lessons. Each lesson has one primary objective, a short bilingual retrieval check, and a dominant learner experience selected for the discourse problem at hand.

| Range | Arc | Dominant evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Detect implication, scrutinise claims, calibrate uncertainty, and reconcile disagreement | Reading, listening, notice, interaction |
| 5–8 | Form proportionate conclusions, connect public policy to lived experience, reason ethically, and challenge a single story | Reading, speaking, writing, listening |
| 9–12 | Define progress, weigh policy costs, design disagreement, and use historical context responsibly | Reading, writing, interaction, review |
| 13–16 | Adapt cultural analysis, attribute expertise, write consequential reviews, and make complexity readable | Listening, reading, writing, assessment |

## Compact lesson architecture

Every lesson exposes only four first-view questions: **What is this? What do I do now? What matters? What follows?** Longer source texts, Arabic explanations, transcripts, word-family notes, and external-AI writing prompts remain progressively disclosed. No lesson is required to contain vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing, speaking, listening, and assessment together.

Each activity includes an English-first objective, a deliberate Arabic support, one dominant action, and a short retrieval/check. Retrieval reconnects to B2 evidence, counterargument, register, consequence, evaluation, and proposal language, but it is only used when the connection is natural.

## Cadence decisions

Reading appears in Lessons 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, and 16, exceeding the C1 every-one-to-two-lesson target without forcing a reading task into the intervening lessons. Writing appears in Lessons 5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 13, 15, and 16, giving frequent synthesis evidence while varying audience and purpose. Listening is used in Lessons 3, 6, 7, 11, 13, and 14, with transcripts disclosed only after an initial listen. Speaking and interaction appear in Lessons 3, 6, 7, 8, 11, 13, and 15. Grammar is embedded as a precision choice rather than taught in consecutive grammar-only blocks.

## Assessment rhythm

Lessons 5, 9, and 15 provide meaningful-use writing evidence. Lesson 12 is a low-pressure retrieval review rather than a generic recap. Lesson 16 is the module checkpoint: the learner must interpret a short specialist dossier, identify evidence weight and uncertainty, adapt the register for a public reader, and recommend a defensible action. External-AI prompts may support drafting and revision, but the course does not send learner work to a paid model and does not pretend to score pronunciation or writing automatically.

## Arabic-speaker support

Arabic support clarifies discourse relationships rather than translating every sentence. Notes explicitly contrast English hedging, attribution, nominalisation, passive reporting, and diplomatic concession with likely Arabic transfer issues. The learner always sees the English target first, can reveal the Arabic explanation, and can return to the source without losing the English context.

## Quality gates

The module passes only when all sixteen lessons have one primary objective, bilingual title and objective, at least one retrieval/check record, a first-view route, an architect-selected archetype, and a supported density. The cadence must remain varied rather than mechanically alternating. Listening lessons must disclose their transcript progressively; writing lessons must remain audience- and task-specific; and the final assessment must sample transfer rather than repeat isolated lesson wording.
