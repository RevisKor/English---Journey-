# C2 Module 5 Architecture — Interpretation, uncertainty, and public meaning

## Scope

C2 Module 5 covers Lessons 61–75. Its arc moves from defending an interpretation to explaining scientific uncertainty, negotiating belonging, leading through crisis, analysing public framing, and translating expertise for a wider audience. The module treats advanced English as **responsible meaning-making**: the learner must distinguish observation from interpretation, preserve uncertainty, and choose language that does not reduce people or evidence.

| Lessons | Arc | Dominant experiences |
|---|---|---|
| 61–63 | Interpretation and evidence | Reading, interaction, listening |
| 64–66 | Public explanation and belonging | Writing, grammar, mediation |
| 67–69 | Crisis language and leadership | Listening, speaking, reading |
| 70–72 | Framing, power, and adaptation | Interaction, grammar, writing |
| 73–75 | Rights, public design, narrative voice, repair, and synthesis | Reading, speaking, review, assessment |

## Authoring principles

Every lesson has one primary objective and one compact retrieval check. Retrieval is bilingual, but the target response remains English-first; Arabic support clarifies the task, not the answer. The activity route changes because the objective changes: there is no fixed reading–writing–speaking–listening rotation.

Reading appears frequently enough for C2, but each passage has a distinct interpretive job: defend an interpretation, identify uncertainty, compare frames, or detect an unreliable voice. Listening routes are audio-first in the interface and disclose the transcript only after an initial attempt. Writing routes provide prompts that can be copied to a learner-selected external AI for feedback; the product does not require a per-request grading API. Interaction and speaking are reserved for negotiation, public explanation, or repair.

The module increases difficulty through **interpretive accountability**, **audience design**, **epistemic calibration**, and **ethical representation**, rather than through longer word lists alone. Lessons 70 and 75 act as a small checkpoint and module transfer assessment respectively. The final assessment asks the learner to mediate a demanding source pack for a defined audience while naming what is known, disputed, and still open to revision.

## Progressive disclosure

The first view answers: what is this, what do I do, what matters, and what comes next. Word support, Arabic explanation, worked examples, extended rationale, and transcripts are revealed only when useful. C2 learners should be challenged by the source or task before seeing its explanatory scaffolding.

## Quality gates

The implementation must provide one authored activity for every lesson in the range, an adaptive experience for every lesson, explicit bilingual retrieval in every activity, at least seven activity kinds across the module, at least four reading routes with checks, at least three writing routes, at least two listening routes with transcript support, and an assessment-semantic final lesson. It must pass TypeScript, production build, curriculum and rearchitecture audits, focused C2 regressions, and the full Vitest suite before checkpointing.

## Assessment intent

Lesson 70 is a compact checkpoint on framing, evidence, and audience. Lesson 75 is the module transfer: the learner receives a short source pack containing an interpretation, a scientific or institutional account, and an affected perspective. The learner produces either a public explainer or a repair-oriented recommendation. Success depends on evidence fidelity, calibrated certainty, ethical representation, register control, cohesion, and a clearly stated revision condition.

## Decision

Module 5 is approved as a **meaning-under-pressure studio**. It extends C2 Module 4's public judgement work while shifting the centre of gravity toward interpretation, uncertainty, discourse framing, and repair.

## References

This packet is grounded in the existing English Journey C2 source inventory and the project's shared authored-activity and adaptive-experience contracts. No external API or per-request AI grading dependency is introduced.
