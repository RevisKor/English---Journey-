# C1 Module 8 Architecture — Culture, Expertise, and Public Meaning

## Module purpose

C1 Module 8 extends the learner's source-aware judgement into questions of culture, expertise, public communication, and the personal consequences of collective decisions. The module is not a repetition of Module 7: it moves from public proposals and accountability toward **how knowledge travels between communities, specialists, institutions, and individual lives**.

The sixteen lessons form a deliberate arc:

> cultural change → limits of expertise → consequential review → readable complexity → pressured proposal → calibrated scepticism → crowded debate → ethical synthesis → implicit meaning → claim scrutiny → uncertainty language → source disagreement → proportionate conclusion → lived public issue → convenience and ethics → beyond a single story.

Each lesson has one primary objective and one short bilingual retrieval check. Activity routes remain compact and archetype-led. Reading appears in several lessons because C1 learners need frequent contact with demanding prose, but writing, listening, speaking, interaction, and review are placed where they serve the arc rather than following a mechanical rotation.

## Skill and retrieval cadence

Reading is used in Lessons 113, 118, 121, 122, and 126, with the final assessment requiring source reading again. Listening is used in Lessons 115, 119, and 124. Writing is used in Lessons 116 and 125, with synthesis writing embedded in Lessons 120 and 128. Interaction and speaking are concentrated in Lessons 117 and 127, with the debate route in Lesson 119 providing an additional spoken-transfer demand. Lesson 120 is the ten-lesson checkpoint. Lesson 128 is the module assessment.

Retrieval checks target transferable evidence moves rather than isolated word recall. Learners retrieve distinctions such as description versus judgement, expertise versus authority, confidence versus certainty, anecdote versus general evidence, and convenience versus sufficient justification. Every retrieval prompt has Arabic support and an expected evidence statement in both languages.

## Progressive disclosure and external-AI policy

Listening transcripts remain hidden by default and are offered only after an initial listening attempt. Reading support uses word-level help, Arabic explanation, and worked examples instead of exposing every explanation at once. Writing activities provide copyable bilingual prompts for an external AI grader; the website itself does not invoke a paid model per request. The learner remains responsible for choosing an external tool and deciding what text to share.

## Assessment architecture

The final assessment uses a fresh dossier about a cultural institution changing its public programme. The learner must compare an institutional review, a specialist commentary, and a personal account; identify what each source can establish; acknowledge a limitation; and write a 360–440-word recommendation with a review trigger. This measures calibrated source handling, public register, ethical representation, and reasoned action rather than memorised lesson content.

## Handoff requirements

The authored corpus must provide one compact activity per lesson, bilingual learner-facing fields, explicit retrieval, reading checks where appropriate, external-AI writing support, listening lines with transcript disclosure, and a distinct assessment record for Lesson 128. The adaptive experience map must provide all four first-view orientation fields and use at least eight archetypes across the module. The C1 pipeline must give Module 8 precedence over earlier generated or authored fallbacks, and the learner-workspace fallback must advance to Lesson 129 after validation.
