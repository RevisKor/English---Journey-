# B2 Module 9 Quality Review

## Scope

This review covers Lessons 121–135, the module architecture packet, the authored activity corpus, the adaptive experience map, and the learner-workspace route. The module is designed as a compact B2 journey through public claims, hidden costs, workplace values, service failures, statistics, access, participation, reform, trust, compromise, attention, equitable progress, and culturally sensitive transfer.

## Pedagogical review

Each lesson has one dominant objective and a short bilingual retrieval check. The sequence is deliberately non-rotational: it moves from claim triage to reading, interaction, listening, statistical reading, writing, practical design, speaking, writing, checkpoint assessment, listening, negotiation, writing, reading, and final assessment. This keeps reading frequent at B2, places writing at a three-to-four-lesson cadence, uses listening in Lessons 124 and 131, and embeds speaking or interaction at appropriate decision points without forcing every skill into every lesson.

The module increases cognitive demand through a clear arc. Early lessons separate evidence from claims; middle lessons require the learner to make and defend decisions for defined audiences; later lessons require synthesis, conditional compromise, trust judgement, and culturally sensitive transfer. Retrieval is explicit rather than decorative: each authored activity asks the learner to produce or identify evidence connected to the lesson objective.

## Visual and disclosure review

The experience map keeps the learner-facing route calm and semantic. Reading, listening, speaking, writing, interaction, real-world, and assessment identities are carried by the existing archetype label and icon system rather than colour alone. The listening routes explicitly include `transcript` as a progressive support, so the workspace can expose audio-first work while keeping the written transcript hidden until it is useful. External-AI writing support remains a prompt-based handoff and does not add an application-side AI cost.

The first-view fields answer the learner's four immediate questions: what the lesson is, what to do, what matters, and what comes next. Longer explanations, word support, Arabic help, worked examples, and external grading prompts remain progressive supports rather than a dense first screen.

## Assessment review

The authored tasks assess transfer rather than recognition alone. Lessons 126 and 129 require audience-aware proposals; Lesson 130 checks calibrated risk response; Lesson 131 checks fair reporting of competing expertise; Lesson 132 checks conditional compromise; Lesson 133 checks causal explanation and alternative design; Lesson 135 checks culturally sensitive translation strategy and objection handling. This gives the module a varied evidence trail across reading, listening, interaction, speaking, and writing.

The assessment language avoids fabricated learner performance, ratings, or testimonials. The course continues to use deterministic question-bank and external-prompt pathways rather than per-request AI grading costs.

## Review conclusion

Module 9 is suitable for final engineering validation. Its route is varied without becoming unpredictable in navigation, its content is compact without being thin, and its retrieval and disclosure controls are explicit. The only remaining release decision is the full validation gate and checkpoint creation.
