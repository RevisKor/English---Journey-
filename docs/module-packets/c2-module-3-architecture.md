# C2 Module 3 Architecture

## Module identity

C2 Module 3, Lessons 31–45, is the **Interpretation Under Pressure Studio**. It revisits the C2 source themes through a higher-order lens: learners must not merely understand difficult material, but decide what can responsibly be inferred, what remains unresolved, and how interpretation changes when the audience, institution, or consequence changes.

The module moves from memory and inherited narratives through markets, institutional trust, art, science, migration, risk, public framing, work, rights, cities, literature, diplomacy, mediation, persuasion, and a synthesis checkpoint. The recurring themes are deliberately treated as extension cycles rather than duplicate vocabulary units. The linguistic work is precision of stance, evidence, implication, attribution, register, rhythm, and ethical framing.

## Learning arc

The opening lessons establish interpretive discipline: distinguish an inherited account from a documented claim, read economic language for what it counts and excludes, and audit institutional language for accountability. The middle of the module asks learners to defend interpretations without closing inquiry, explain uncertainty without collapsing into vagueness, speak about migration and belonging without reduction, and communicate risk with proportionate confidence. The final lessons transfer those habits into public narratives, lifelong learning, competing rights, urban design, unreliable narration, diplomacy, expertise mediation, and ethical persuasion.

By Lesson 45, the learner produces a defensible public position that makes its interpretive choices, evidence boundaries, affected audiences, and rhetorical risks visible.

## Objective-led archetype balance

The architect chooses the dominant archetype from the local communicative problem, not a fixed rotation. The intended balance across fifteen lessons is approximately four source-mediated reading or interpretation routes, three interaction or negotiation routes, three listening or speaking routes, three writing or revision routes, one review clinic, and one synthesis assessment. Grammar appears as a precision tool inside relevant routes, especially stance, attribution, concession, modality, information structure, and cohesion.

| Design target | Module 3 decision |
|---|---|
| Primary objective | One advanced communicative decision per lesson. |
| Retrieval | Every lesson opens with a bilingual retrieval prompt connected to a prior C1/C2 idea. |
| Reading | Repeated source comparison, reliability, framing, and evidence-limit work. |
| Writing | Audience-specific explanation, critique, mediation, and public-position writing. |
| Listening | Authentic-feeling institutional, scientific, crisis, and diplomatic speech with transcripts hidden by default. |
| Speaking/interaction | Negotiation, repair, briefing, interpretation defense, and ethical persuasion. |
| Review | One deliberate clinic consolidates stance, attribution, and reader/listener effort. |
| Assessment | Lesson 45 is a transfer task requiring synthesis and independent judgement. |

## Retrieval and disclosure

Retrieval is explicit but compact. Prompts ask learners to retrieve a useful distinction, phrase, or reasoning move rather than recite an isolated definition. Arabic prompts clarify the intended operation without replacing the English work. Each record supplies expected evidence in both languages.

Progressive disclosure is essential at C2. Learners first encounter the source, audio, prompt, or dilemma without every explanation visible. Transcript, translation support, extended model, rhetorical annotation, and external-AI writing prompt appear as second-pass supports. No lesson is required to expose every content field at once.

## Assessment intent

The final assessment is not a grammar survey. It asks learners to assemble a multi-source position, state the proposition, qualify uncertainty, represent competing rights or interests, adapt the language for a public audience, and add a short transparency note explaining omissions and rhetorical choices. Passing evidence must demonstrate precision, coherence, ethical attribution, audience awareness, and controlled style.

## Engineering and review gates

The authored corpus must conform to the shared `LessonActivity` and `LessonExperience` contracts. Focused tests will check complete lesson coverage, bilingual retrieval, archetype diversity, source-mediated reading, writing prompts, transcript disclosure, interaction purposes, adaptive first-view orientation, and transfer assessment. The full gate remains TypeScript, production build, curriculum audit, rearchitecture audit, and the complete Vitest suite.
