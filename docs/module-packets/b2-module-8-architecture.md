# B2 Module 8 Architecture Packet

## Scope and source

This packet governs B2 Lessons 106–120. The lesson titles and outcomes below are taken from `shared/course/b2-draft.json`, the authoritative B2 draft source. The module extends B2 argumentation into risk, expertise, belonging, technology, work, heritage, and public-choice decisions. It is not a rotation of skills: each lesson has one primary job, a short retrieval/check, and only the supporting stages required by that job.

## Module purpose

The learner moves from evaluating a single claim to defending a qualified position for a real audience. The sequence deliberately alternates evidence interpretation, spoken interaction, listening/reading, concise writing, and synthesis so the learner repeatedly weighs trade-offs without receiving the same activity pattern in every lesson. Arabic support is selective: it clarifies abstract distinctions, discourse moves, and culturally sensitive wording, rather than translating every advanced passage.

## Governing controls

| Control | Module 8 decision |
|---|---|
| Primary objective | One concrete interpret, decide, say, read, or write outcome per lesson. |
| Retrieval | Every lesson includes one short bilingual retrieval/check tied to the new decision or discourse move. |
| Density | Light lessons prepare or retrieve; normal lessons develop one argument move; deep lessons are reserved for synthesis and the module checkpoint. |
| Skill cadence | Reading appears frequently at B2; writing recurs every few lessons; listening and speaking are distinct and recur across the module; interaction and real-world tasks appear where the objective requires them. These are balancing ranges, not a fixed rotation. |
| Omission rule | A lesson may omit reading, writing, listening, speaking, grammar, or vocabulary when the omission protects focus. |
| Assessment | Lesson 113 is an evidence-and-counterargument checkpoint; Lesson 120 is the module assessment and requires a concise persuasive response. |
| Disclosure | First view states what the lesson is, what to do, what matters, and what comes next. Transcripts, Arabic help, worked examples, and extended rationale remain progressive disclosures. |

## Retrieval and progression map

Retrieval is intentionally local and purposeful. Lessons 106–108 establish calibrated claims, trust, and compromise; 109–112 transfer those moves into media, access, translation, and belonging; 113 checks evidence and rebuttal; 114–119 extend the same reasoning into technology, reviews, disagreement, policy, work, and preservation; Lesson 120 requires independent transfer in a concise persuasive response. The map therefore revisits discourse moves without forcing old vocabulary into unnatural contexts.

## Research and learner-context notes

B2 Arabic-speaking learners need explicit contrast between certainty and possibility, stance reporting, concession, diplomatic disagreement, formal register, and evidence language. Common risks include treating hedging as weakness, overusing direct disagreement, confusing a source’s claim with the writer’s position, and producing recommendations without a stated trade-off. The activity corpus addresses these risks through selective Arabic explanations, worked examples, and visible evidence prompts.

## Handoff decision

The packet passes the architecture gate when every lesson has a traceable authoritative outcome, one dominant archetype, a density rationale, a deliberate stage selection, a short retrieval/check, and an evidence route. Content authoring must preserve the compact scope shown here and must not add every skill to every lesson merely to increase visible content.
