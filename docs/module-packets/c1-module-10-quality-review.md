# C1 Module 10 Quality Review

## Scope

C1 Module 10 covers Lessons 145–160 and closes the C1 journey with a synthesis-and-public-communication arc. The module moves from visible distributional consequences and precise evaluative language through ethical interaction, representation, measurement, allocation, deliberation, historical transfer, cultural framing, expertise boundaries, public clarity, conditional proposals, epistemic humility, and a final conflicting-sources judgement challenge.

The module remains compact and objective-led. Each lesson has one dominant purpose, bilingual learner-facing orientation, an explicit retrieval check, and a deliberately selected experience rather than a universal stage sequence.

## Lesson-journey review

The lesson sequence is intentionally non-uniform. Lesson 145 begins with a reading studio on unequal benefits and hidden transition costs. Lesson 146 isolates proportion and context in grammar before Lesson 147 turns the language into an ethical-technology exchange. Lessons 148–149 use representation and metrics to widen the learner’s evidence frame. Lesson 150 asks for a formal allocation proposal. Lessons 151–153 then move through deliberation, historical transfer, and audio-first cultural framing. Lessons 154–157 address expertise boundaries, consequential review writing, public clarity, and rapid conditional proposals. Lessons 158–159 return to calibrated certainty through reading and listening. Lesson 160 integrates the entire module into a reversible, source-aware public judgement.

This progression gives the learner a coherent final-C1 story: **notice consequences, qualify claims, hear disagreement, preserve agency, match methods to questions, communicate clearly, and revise responsibly**.

## Visual and disclosure review

The experience map supplies `whatItIs`, `whatToDo`, `whatMatters`, and `whatNext` for every lesson so the learner can answer the four orientation questions immediately. Density varies between normal and deep. Progressive supports expose word support, Arabic help, worked examples, extended rationale, transcript disclosure, and external-AI prompts only where the lesson requires them.

Listening routes in Lessons 153 and 159 retain transcript support as a progressive disclosure rather than presenting the transcript as the first task. Writing routes distinguish the learner’s work from the optional external-AI feedback prompt. Assessment remains visually and semantically distinct through the `assessment` archetype and explicit transfer objective.

The module inherits the calm neutral workspace language: color identifies semantic activity types, while bilingual labels, route descriptions, and progressive supports carry meaning. Rich source content is stored in the activity corpus but is revealed through staged experience routes.

## Assessment review

Lesson 160 is a genuine transfer assessment rather than a vocabulary recall exercise. It asks the learner to attribute at least four sources, distinguish aggregate benefit from distributional cost, identify expert-method limits, qualify a historical comparison, represent dissent, and recommend a reversible action with a concrete review trigger. The assessment therefore measures source handling, register, synthesis, uncertainty, and public responsibility together.

Retrieval is embedded in the assessment sequence: the learner must recover the final C1 moves—measure, distribute, attribute, qualify, deliberate, revise—and apply them to a fresh conflicting-sources dossier. This preserves the project’s external-feedback model while avoiding fabricated grading claims or an in-product AI dependency.
