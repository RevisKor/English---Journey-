# B2 Module 10 Quality Review

## Review scope

This review covers Lessons 136–150, the final B2 module in the current 150-lesson authored sequence. The review checks whether the module preserves the six-agent operating system: one primary objective per lesson, bilingual learner-facing guidance, explicit retrieval, varied archetypes, progressive disclosure, and a balanced but non-rotational skill cadence.

## Pedagogical review

Module 10 is framed as **Judgement, Responsibility, and Public Decisions**. The sequence moves from evaluating belonging and public claims to constructing policy arguments, questioning automated decisions, negotiating workplace trade-offs, assessing development choices, verifying contested information, and completing an ethical decision assessment. Each lesson has one dominant action rather than a compressed survey of vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing, speaking, and listening.

| Lesson range | Dominant route | Primary learning move | Quality judgement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 136–139 | Reading and evaluation | Separate social value, motive, evidence, and qualified recommendation | Concrete opening; light retrieval before production |
| 140–143 | Speaking, writing, interaction, real-world | Reformulate, challenge, predict, negotiate, and conditionally compromise | Varied public and workplace contexts; no mechanical rotation |
| 144–149 | Persuasive writing, listening, reading | Concede, verify, qualify, infer, and interpret system evidence | Strong transfer from public prose to audio-first source checking |
| 150 | Assessment | Make and justify an ethical decision with safeguards and counterfactual reasoning | Appropriate cumulative endpoint for B2 Module 10 |

The module increases cognitive demand through transfer rather than simply increasing text length. Learners repeatedly distinguish evidence from claim, preserve opposing views before challenging them, and convert judgement into a proportionate recommendation. The final assessment gathers these moves without requiring every earlier activity pattern to appear in the same lesson.

## Retrieval and cadence review

Every authored activity includes an explicit bilingual retrieval check. Retrieval is distributed through reading verdicts, source verification, consequence prediction, workplace negotiation, and the pre-assessment statistic lesson. The module uses a dedicated cumulative assessment at Lesson 150 and avoids forcing unrelated earlier vocabulary into artificial contexts.

The cadence is intentionally varied: reading occurs at Lessons 136, 139, 148, and 149; writing at 137, 141, 144, and 146; listening at 145; speaking at 140 and 147; and interaction or real-world decision work at 138, 142, and 143. The sequence is not a fixed reading–writing–speaking–listening rotation: two reading lessons establish evidence work, production then branches into ethical interaction, workplace negotiation, and public writing, and the later audio route changes the mode before assessment.

## Bilingual and learner-support review

Titles, objectives, prompts, readings, dialogue lines, and retrieval checks are paired in English and Arabic. The first-view experience map answers the four learner questions: what the lesson is, what to do, what matters, and what comes next. Arabic support is available as a progressive support rather than replacing the English-first objective. External-AI writing support remains prompt-based and carries no per-request application AI cost.

## Visual and disclosure review

Listening Lesson 145 is audio-first in the shared activity contract, with transcript disclosure represented as a progressive support rather than a first-view block. Reading and writing lessons expose the task objective and response shape first; extended rationale, worked examples, and external-AI prompts can be revealed when needed. The module therefore stores rich bilingual content without presenting every explanation at once.

The semantic roles remain distinguishable through the established label-plus-icon visual language: examples for source text, grammar for writing frames, activity for interaction, retrieval for checks, and assessment for the final decision. No new decorative visual system is required for Module 10.

## Assessment review

The module includes multiple evidence forms before the final assessment: critical reading, public argument, spoken reformulation, conditional negotiation, source verification, passive reporting, statistic interpretation, and an ethical writing task. Lesson 150 asks the learner to evaluate competing duties, choose a present course of action, use mixed or counterfactual conditionals, and propose safeguards. This is appropriately broader than a lesson quiz while retaining one primary outcome: justified ethical decision-making.

The external-AI grading route should evaluate evidence use, fair representation of alternatives, cohesion, grammatical control, lexical precision, and response to the prompt. The curriculum itself does not fabricate scores or reviews; it supplies transparent criteria and a learner-generated response task.

## Risks and follow-up controls

The principal implementation risk is contract drift in authored activity metadata because the shared course contracts distinguish lesson type, archetype, and progression stage. The engineering gate must therefore include TypeScript and both curriculum audits. The standing external database SSL startup warning is unrelated to Module 10 content and should remain documented separately from curriculum quality.

## Review decision

**Module 10 is pedagogically ready for engineering validation.** It is compact, bilingual, varied, retrieval-aware, and suitable as the final authored B2 module. Proceed to the full typecheck, production build, curriculum audit, rearchitecture audit, focused regression suite, shared-course suite, and learner-workspace validation before checkpointing.
