# C2 Module 10 Architecture: The Capstone Studio

## Scope and promise

Lessons 136–150 form the final authored C2 module. Earlier modules developed precision, mediation, conflict interpretation, institutional judgement, uncertainty, risk, rights, narrative reliability, and accountable deliberation. The capstone therefore does not introduce another isolated topic. It asks the learner to bring those resources together in a consequential public task.

The guiding question is: **How can I make a complex position understandable, defensible, revisable, and humane for a real audience?** The arc moves from synthesis and audience design to public explanation, disagreement management, revision under challenge, and a final C2 position that can withstand scrutiny.

| Band | Lessons | Dominant work |
|---|---:|---|
| Synthesis | 136–139 | map evidence, choose a position, control register, and design for audience effort |
| Public mediation | 140–143 | explain complexity, respond to challenge, negotiate disagreement, and speak with authority without overclaiming |
| Revision and transfer | 144–147 | revise after critique, make uncertainty visible, translate across audiences, and preserve ethical boundaries |
| Capstone | 148–150 | rehearse, review, and submit a multimodal C2 position dossier |

## Archetype and cadence decisions

The module deliberately alternates between synthesis, reading, grammar, listening, interaction, speaking, writing, review, and assessment according to the capstone’s dependency structure. It does not use a fixed rotation. Reading appears where the learner needs a model or source pack; listening appears before public speaking; writing appears after evidence and audience decisions are available; review lessons consolidate choices rather than merely repeat vocabulary.

Every lesson has one primary objective and one bilingual retrieval check. The final three lessons make the success criteria progressively more visible: rehearsal, accountable revision, and independent submission. Listening transcripts remain progressive supports. External-AI prompts are supplied for optional grading and revision only; no per-request AI call is required by the site.

## Capstone success criteria

A successful final dossier or briefing will distinguish evidence from inference, calibrate certainty, represent affected perspectives, make a value choice explicit, anticipate a serious objection, use C2 lexical and grammatical control for a defined audience, and state how the position should be reviewed if conditions change. The learner is evaluated on communicative effect and intellectual responsibility, not on ornamental complexity.
