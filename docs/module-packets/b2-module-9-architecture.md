# B2 Module 9 Architecture Packet

## Scope and source

This packet governs B2 Lessons 121–135. The lesson titles, outcomes, and scenarios are taken from `docs/module-packets/b2-module-9-source.json`, extracted from `shared/course/b2-draft.json`, the authoritative B2 draft source. Module 9 is designed as **Public Choices and Shared Futures**: learners interpret contested information, examine who benefits from systems and decisions, and communicate qualified proposals to mixed audiences. It is not a fixed rotation of skills. Each lesson has one dominant learner job, one short bilingual retrieval/check, and only the supporting stages needed for that job.

## Module purpose

The learner moves from evaluating public claims to proposing fair, practical changes. The module revisits B2 discourse moves—qualification, concession, evidence, consequence, diplomatic disagreement, and recommendation—but transfers them into new settings: convenience, employment, infrastructure, participation, media attention, access, expertise, compromise, risk, and translation. Arabic support is selective and purposeful: it clarifies abstract distinctions, audience-sensitive phrasing, and likely Arabic-speaker transfer risks without translating every advanced text.

## Governing controls

| Control | Module 9 decision |
|---|---|
| Primary objective | One concrete interpret, evaluate, listen, negotiate, speak, read, or write outcome per lesson. |
| Retrieval | Every lesson includes one brief bilingual retrieval/check tied to the lesson's main discourse move or decision. |
| Density | Light lessons orient or retrieve; normal lessons develop one transferable move; deep lessons reserve space for synthesis, source comparison, or assessment. |
| Skill cadence | Reading appears every 1–2 lessons where the objective calls for source interpretation; writing recurs at Lessons 126, 129, and 133; listening is audio-first at Lessons 124 and 131; speaking and interaction recur through Lessons 123, 128, and 132. This is a balancing range, not a fixed rotation. |
| Omission rule | A lesson may omit reading, writing, listening, speaking, grammar, or vocabulary when omission protects the primary objective. |
| Assessment | Lesson 130 is the ten-lesson checkpoint; Lesson 135 is the module assessment and requires independent transfer across the module's public-choice language. |
| Disclosure | The first view states what the lesson is, what to do, what matters, and what comes next. Transcripts, Arabic support, worked examples, and extended rationale are progressively disclosed. |
| Context integrity | Previous B2 language is retrieved only when it serves the new decision. New vocabulary is introduced in compact, source-bound clusters rather than broad lists. |

## Retrieval and progression map

Lessons 121–125 establish the module's information discipline: claim versus evidence, hidden cost, workplace values, system consequences, and statistic versus conclusion. Lessons 126–129 transfer those moves into decision notes, accessible design, participation, and reform proposals. Lesson 130 checks the first ten-lesson arc through a qualified risk response. Lessons 131–134 widen the audience and complexity through expert trust, negotiated compromise, attention policy, and equitable innovation. Lesson 135 asks for independent transfer into culturally sensitive translation, where the learner must weigh fidelity, identity, audience, and practical consequence rather than repeat a memorised structure.

Retrieval checks remain short because the main evidence belongs to the lesson archetype. A learner may be asked to name the missing evidence, select the stronger qualification, reformulate a stakeholder's priority, identify the consequence chain in audio, distinguish a measured statistic from an implied claim, or retrieve a concession before writing. These checks revisit discourse moves without forcing old vocabulary into unnatural contexts.

## Research and learner-context notes

B2 Arabic-speaking learners benefit from explicit contrast between evidence and assertion, direct and qualified disagreement, individual convenience and collective cost, and literal translation and culturally appropriate meaning. Likely transfer risks include treating a percentage as self-explanatory, assuming expert status proves a claim, presenting accessibility as an optional extra, overusing categorical language in public writing, and translating idioms word for word. Module 9 addresses these risks with source-bound examples, selective Arabic explanations, audience labels, and visible evidence prompts.

The module also increases authentic listening without adding an external media dependency. Lessons 124 and 131 use the existing audio-first activity contract with browser-native playback and a transcript hidden by default. The learner first listens for structure or stance, then opens the transcript only for verification or language noticing. This preserves the calm visual system and prevents the transcript from replacing listening.

## Handoff decision

The packet passes the architecture gate when every lesson has a traceable authoritative outcome, one dominant archetype, a density rationale, a deliberate stage selection, a bilingual retrieval/check, and a learner-evidence route. Content authoring must preserve the compact scope shown here. Authors must not add every skill to every lesson merely to increase visible content; any added activity must directly support the primary learner job.
