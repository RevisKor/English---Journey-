# B2 Module 6 Architecture Packet — Lessons 76–90

## Six-agent handoff status

This packet records the research and architecture handoff for B2 Module 6. The authoritative source is `shared/course/b2-draft.json`, restricted to lesson records 76–90. The module is designed as a varied sequence of compact, objective-led journeys rather than a universal skill bundle.

## Module purpose

Module 6 develops careful public reasoning: institutional responsibility, statistics, ethical decisions, inclusive design, participation, reform, risk, expertise, compromise, attention, access, translation, belonging, argument, and automated decision-making. The B2 learner is expected to qualify claims, evaluate evidence, anticipate consequences, and propose reviewable action.

The module deliberately moves between evidence-reading, interaction, grammar-in-use, listening, speaking, writing, review, and assessment. These are balancing decisions, not a rotation. Each lesson has one primary objective, a short bilingual retrieval/check, and only the supporting stages needed for that objective.

## Cadence and compactness controls

Reading is frequent at B2 but appears here only where it serves evidence interpretation (77, 83, 86). Writing appears at 79, 81, and 88, with major assessment writing at 89–90. Listening appears at 82 and 87 with transcript disclosure rather than forced transcription. Speaking and interaction are separated across 78, 80, and 84. Grammar is spaced at 76 and 85 rather than placed in consecutive dominant lessons. Review is embedded through short retrieval and the negotiation/transfer lessons; the module assessment at 90 is the required end-of-module gate.

No lesson is required to contain every skill. The `retrievalCheck` is deliberately brief: one prompt, one expected evidence statement, and Arabic support. Rich source data remains behind progressive disclosure. First-view metadata must answer: what is this, what do I do, what matters, and what comes next.

## Arabic-speaking learner decisions

Arabic support is used for task orientation, key distinctions, and common risks, not as a full parallel translation of every advanced passage. Particular care is given to passive responsibility, hedging, nominalisation, cleft emphasis, mixed conditionals, reporting stance, and diplomatic disagreement. The learner is repeatedly asked to distinguish grammatical form from communicative force.

## Assessment map

Lessons 81 and 89 provide substantial evidence-writing checkpoints; Lesson 90 is the module assessment. Assessment prompts require evidence, a stated position, a counterargument or trade-off, and a reviewable safeguard or measure. Distractors and prompts must not reward memorising one phrase. The assessment review must confirm that the learner can transfer the module’s reasoning patterns to a new B2 public context.

## Gate 2 decision

**Pass with controls.** The module has a purpose-led arc, varied archetypes, compact scope, explicit retrieval, distinct listening disclosure, and a proportionate end-of-module assessment. Content authoring may proceed only while preserving the lesson-level primary-objective rule and the cadence as a balancing range rather than a rigid rotation.
