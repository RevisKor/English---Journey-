# B2 Module 10 Architecture: Judgement, Institutions, and Difficult Choices

## Module purpose

B2 Module 10 closes the current B2 sequence by moving the learner from public persuasion into independent judgement. Lessons 136–150 revisit belonging, evidence, automated decisions, disagreement, work, preservation, contested information, convenience, institutional failure, statistics, and ethical choice. The module is not a template repeated fifteen times: each lesson selects one dominant learner experience and makes the next lesson a meaningful bridge.

The learner should leave the module able to evaluate competing claims, report sources with appropriate hedging, reformulate another person's position fairly, recommend a practical plan, and reason about consequences without overclaiming. Arabic support remains visible in titles, objectives, prompts, and key explanations; the English-first progression remains appropriate for B2.

## Architecture principles

Every lesson has one primary objective and one short bilingual retrieval/check. Activities are compact enough to complete in one sitting, but deep lessons may require sustained reading or writing. Rich source data is progressively disclosed: the first view answers what the lesson is, what the learner should do, what matters, and what comes next; word support, worked examples, Arabic help, transcripts, and external-AI prompts appear only when needed.

The module uses frequency targets as safeguards, not as a rigid rotation. Reading is concentrated in Lessons 136, 139, 145, and 149; writing appears in Lessons 137, 141, 144, 146, 148, and the final assessment; listening is used where source comparison benefits from audio; interaction and speaking are used to rehearse disagreement, workplace values, and ethical choice. Grammar is embedded in the communicative objective rather than taught as a consecutive block.

| Lesson range | Dominant arc | Learner evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 136–140 | Belonging, argument, automation, reviews, disagreement | Evaluate, rebut, reformulate, and propose |
| 141–144 | Consequences, work, preservation, defended position | Plan, negotiate, and write for a real reader |
| 145–150 | Information, convenience, institutions, statistics, ethics | Verify, qualify, report responsibility, and reason counterfactually |

## Retrieval and assessment rhythm

Each authored activity receives a short English/Arabic retrieval record asking the learner to retrieve a phrase, distinction, or reasoning move from the current lesson. Lesson 140 functions as a local discourse checkpoint; Lesson 144 consolidates the first nine lessons; Lesson 150 is the module assessment. The final assessment must accept justified variation in wording while checking evidence use, calibrated claims, ethical reasoning, and consequence awareness.

## Visual and engineering contract

The module uses the shared semantic visual language: archetype icon and label, calm neutral surfaces, compact objective header, progressive supports, and a clear next bridge. Listening routes disclose transcripts only after the learner attempts the audio. Writing routes provide copyable external-AI grading prompts rather than invoking an application-side model. All authored records must satisfy the shared activity type, bilingual text, retrieval-check, and experience-map contracts, and the first un-authored fallback advances to Lesson 151 only after validation.
