# English Journey A1 Curriculum Review Specification

**Status:** Review draft only. This document is the proposed A1 curriculum architecture; it has **not** been migrated into the active learner catalog. The current five-lesson active modules should remain unchanged until this specification is approved.

## Executive decision requested

Please review the module themes, lesson count, lesson rhythm, vocabulary progression, grammar scope, and assessment expectations below. The proposed A1 track contains **six modules with fifteen lessons each: 90 active lessons in total**. This is intentionally more substantial than the current five-lesson module structure. The architecture is designed around repeated communication rather than one-time vocabulary exposure.

The design follows the Council of Europe’s A1 descriptors: learners work with concrete personal needs, very basic expressions, clear and slow language, short directions, simple one-clause structures, familiar topics, and highly supported interaction [1]. It also follows the Cambridge A1 pattern of integrating listening, speaking, reading, and writing around familiar topics, thematic vocabulary, explicit grammar structures, and repeated reinforcement [2]. Oxford’s A1 course positioning is used as a reference for a coherent adult beginner syllabus rather than a collection of disconnected grammar pages [3].

> **Core product principle:** A learner should not merely see *apple* once and answer a multiple-choice question. The learner should meet *apple* in a picture, hear it, repeat it, use it in a sentence, encounter it in a dialogue, read it in a short text, write it, retrieve it later, and use it in a meaningful task.

## 1. The A1 learner promise

At the end of A1, a learner who began with almost no English should be able to handle familiar, concrete situations with support. The learner should introduce themselves, exchange simple personal information, identify people and objects, describe a home and family, talk about food and preferences, describe a simple daily routine, ask for and understand basic services, talk about jobs and free-time activities, and manage short real-life interactions.

The learner is **not** expected to understand abstract grammar terminology, sustain a long essay, infer sophisticated implied meaning, or speak spontaneously about unfamiliar topics. A1 content should be respectful but concrete. The mentor explains the reason for a pattern before naming it, uses English examples with Arabic support, and gradually reduces Arabic as the learner gains control.

| A1 outcome area | End-of-level learner can-do target | Evidence collected in the course |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | Understand clear, slow questions, short instructions, familiar words, and short everyday dialogues. | Listen-and-point tasks, repeat-after-me, dialogue checks, short audio comprehension. |
| Speaking | Give simple personal information, describe familiar people and objects, express basic likes, and complete short transactions. | Recorded repetition, sentence building, role-play, guided speaking checkpoints. |
| Reading | Understand signs, labels, short factual descriptions, simple messages, and very short familiar stories with support. | Picture-supported readings, notices, messages, comprehension questions. |
| Writing | Copy and spell high-frequency words, complete forms, write simple sentences, and produce short connected paragraphs about familiar topics. | Controlled writing, sentence assembly, short personal paragraphs, module writing tasks. |
| Grammar | Use basic structures as tools for meaning, including **be**, pronouns, articles, plurals, possessives, present simple, there is/are, can, questions, imperatives, and basic prepositions. | Guided practice, contextual quizzes, correction tasks, cumulative tests. |
| Vocabulary | Build a practical bank of approximately 500–600 high-frequency and domain-specific words and chunks, with Arabic meaning, pronunciation, example, and source lesson. | Word-bank review, picture retrieval, collocation tasks, cumulative vocabulary checks. |

## 2. Proposed A1 structure

The A1 route contains **six modules**. Each module has exactly **15 lessons**, with eleven teaching-and-practice lessons, two review or retrieval lessons, one milestone checkpoint, and one cumulative module test. The final two lessons are not filler: the milestone checks the most recent lesson family, while the module test checks cumulative use across the whole module.

| Module | Theme | Lessons | Main communicative outcome | Vocabulary scale |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Meeting People and Building a First English Toolkit | 15 | Introduce yourself, ask names, identify people, and use basic classroom language. | 75–90 items |
| 2 | My World: Numbers, Colours, Family, and Home | 15 | Describe people, possessions, rooms, locations, and simple quantities. | 80–95 items |
| 3 | Food, Fruit, Vegetables, and Everyday Choices | 15 | Name food, express likes and needs, shop simply, and describe meals. | 85–100 items |
| 4 | Time, Daily Life, and Healthy Habits | 15 | Say the time, describe routines, ask about frequency, and give simple advice. | 80–95 items |
| 5 | People at Work, Places in Town, and Useful Services | 15 | Name jobs and places, ask for directions, and manage simple service interactions. | 85–100 items |
| 6 | Hobbies, Sports, Weather, and Real-Life Interaction | 15 | Talk about free time and weather, invite someone, make plans, and complete an A1 survival interaction. | 90–105 items |
| **Total** |  | **90** |  | **495–585 items** |

Vocabulary counts are ranges rather than a promise to inflate the word bank. A single lexical item can be introduced once but intentionally revisited several times. The word bank should distinguish **new item**, **recycled item**, **fixed expression**, and **grammar frame** so progress is not confused with raw item count.

## 3. Standard fifteen-lesson rhythm

Every module follows a recognizable rhythm so the learner knows what to expect. The content changes, but the learning loop remains stable.

| Lesson | Primary mode | Purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Mentor orientation + vocabulary | Enter the theme through a short bilingual mentor message and learn the first concrete words. |
| 2 | Visual vocabulary | See, hear, identify, and classify the new words using pictures or simple visual cards. |
| 3 | Grammar discovery | Notice one useful pattern in examples before learning its simple name. |
| 4 | Controlled grammar practice | Build positive, negative, and question forms with guided choices. |
| 5 | Dialogue and interaction | Read and hear a short real-life exchange using the first vocabulary and grammar frame. |
| 6 | Speaking / repeat-after-me | Listen, repeat, replay, and join short lines into a complete exchange. |
| 7 | Vocabulary network | Add related words, opposites, categories, or word families and retrieve earlier items. |
| 8 | Reading | Read a short A1 text, message, notice, profile, menu, or story with visual support. |
| 9 | Reading-to-language practice | Answer contextual questions and notice how target grammar carries meaning. |
| 10 | Writing workshop | Assemble sentences, copy a model, then produce a short guided text. |
| 11 | Real-life task | Use the language in a practical role-play or information-gap activity. |
| 12 | Retrieval review | Review the module word bank through mixed recognition, recall, pronunciation, and context. |
| 13 | Supported production | Complete a longer speaking or writing task using earlier module language. |
| 14 | Milestone checkpoint | Complete a level-appropriate checkpoint covering recent vocabulary, grammar, reading, and interaction. |
| 15 | Cumulative module test | Retrieve the complete module, complete an integrated task, and receive a next-step recommendation. |

Lessons 14 and 15 must not be reduced to “eight random questions.” Lesson 14 is a formative milestone with feedback and retry guidance. Lesson 15 is the progression gate: it should include contextual vocabulary, grammar-in-use, short reading, a practical response, and a small production task. The learner may review the relevant word-bank entries before retrying.

## 4. Module 1 — Meeting People and Building a First English Toolkit

**Mentor arc:** “You do not need to know everything today. We will build a small toolkit and use it immediately. First learn how to recognize people and greetings; then use those pieces to introduce yourself.” Arabic support is strongest in Lessons 1–5, balanced in Lessons 6–10, and used mainly for difficult instructions or reflection in Lessons 11–15.

| L | Lesson title | Mode | New language and learner action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hello, Goodbye, and Welcome | Orientation + vocabulary | Learn hello, hi, goodbye, bye, please, thank you, sorry, and welcome. Match each expression to a social moment. |
| 2 | My Name Is… | Visual vocabulary | Learn name, first name, last name, person, man, woman, boy, girl. Hear and identify people in simple picture cards. |
| 3 | The Verb **Be**: I Am and You Are | Grammar discovery | Understand **am/is/are** as the English way to connect a person with information. Build “I am Yahya” and “You are a student.” |
| 4 | Saying No and Asking a Question | Controlled grammar | Make negatives and questions: “I am not…”, “Are you…?”, “Yes, I am / No, I’m not.” Explain that English needs the verb in the question. |
| 5 | A First Introduction | Dialogue | Follow a slow dialogue: greeting, name, country, and goodbye. Identify who says each line and choose an appropriate reply. |
| 6 | Repeat the Introduction | Speaking | Repeat each line, replay difficult lines, then perform the full two-person dialogue. Focus on sentence rhythm, not accent perfection. |
| 7 | Countries and Languages | Vocabulary network | Add country, city, language, Arabic, English, British, American, from, live, speak. Reuse “I am from…” and “I speak…” |
| 8 | A Simple Learner Profile | Reading | Read a 45–60 word profile with name, age, city, country, and language. Use names and numbers as reading anchors. |
| 9 | What Does the Profile Say? | Reading-to-language | Answer who/where/what questions and highlight **is**, **am**, **are**, and **from** in context. |
| 10 | Write Your First Profile | Writing | Complete a scaffold, then write 4–6 sentences: name, age, city, country, language, and one like. |
| 11 | Classroom English | Interaction | Use “Can you repeat?”, “I don’t understand,” “How do you say…?”, “Open your book,” and “Look here.” |
| 12 | Word-Bank Retrieval 1 | Review | Retrieve greetings, identity words, countries, numbers, and classroom phrases through audio, pictures, Arabic prompts, and sentence completion. |
| 13 | Meet Three People | Supported production | Read three name cards and conduct a guided introduction. The learner must ask and answer at least four questions. |
| 14 | Module 1 Milestone | Assessment | Contextual vocabulary, **be** forms, greetings, a short listening dialogue, profile reading, and a 5-sentence personal response. |
| 15 | Module 1 Test: My First Conversation | Cumulative test | Complete a short introduction role-play, a profile reading, grammar-in-context items, and a written introduction. Pass gate: 80% on objective items plus task completion. |

**Grammar depth for Module 1:** explain subject as “the person or thing we are talking about” rather than assuming the term. Explain that English normally needs a subject and a verb. Show positive, negative, and question forms in a three-column visual. Include Arabic-speaker guidance: Arabic can omit the present-tense equivalent of **be**, while English normally cannot in sentences such as “I happy”; English requires “I am happy.” Teach contractions only after the full forms are understood.

**Core repeated exposure:** *hello, goodbye, please, thank you, name, person, man, woman, student, English, Arabic, from, live, speak, city, country, understand*. Each must appear in at least four modes: visual, dialogue, reading, and production.

## 5. Module 2 — My World: Numbers, Colours, Family, and Home

**Mentor arc:** “Now English becomes a way to point to the world around you. We will describe people, count things, and say where things are.” The module introduces **noun**, **singular**, and **plural** only through examples first: “one book / two books.” Arabic explanations distinguish the English plural ending from Arabic plural patterns without requiring linguistic terminology.

| L | Lesson title | Mode | New language and learner action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Numbers 0–20 and Personal Details | Vocabulary | Learn numbers, age, phone, number, and how to say “I am twenty.” Complete a simple learner card. |
| 2 | Colours and Size | Visual vocabulary | Learn red, blue, green, yellow, black, white, big, small, long, short. Describe picture objects. |
| 3 | One Thing or More Than One | Grammar discovery | Notice singular/plural nouns and **a/an**: a book, an apple, two books. Explain vowel sound simply through examples. |
| 4 | This, That, These, Those | Controlled grammar | Point to near and far objects; use “This is…” and “These are…” with picture cards. |
| 5 | Family Words | Vocabulary + dialogue | Learn mother, father, parent, brother, sister, child, husband, wife, family, friend. Follow a family introduction. |
| 6 | My Family Speaking Card | Speaking | Repeat and perform “This is my…” lines. Describe two people with name, age, and one simple adjective. |
| 7 | Possession: My, Your, His, Her | Grammar discovery | Use possessive words in “my mother,” “your book,” “his name,” “her bag.” Contrast possession with the person who owns something. |
| 8 | Rooms and Furniture | Visual vocabulary | Learn home, house, room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, door, table, chair, bed, window, book, bag. Label a picture. |
| 9 | Where Is It? | Grammar + reading | Learn in, on, under, next to, and behind. Read a 60–80 word room description and locate objects. |
| 10 | My Home Paragraph | Writing | Build and write 5–7 sentences about rooms and objects using there is/there are with a model. |
| 11 | At Home with a Visitor | Interaction | Welcome a visitor, offer a seat, identify family members, and answer simple questions about the home. |
| 12 | Word-Bank Retrieval 2 | Review | Retrieve numbers, colours, family, possession, rooms, furniture, and location phrases. Include pronunciation and spelling checks without trick distractors. |
| 13 | Describe the Picture | Supported production | Give a guided description of a family-home picture using at least eight target items and three location phrases. |
| 14 | Module 2 Milestone | Assessment | Numbers, colours, plural forms, possessives, there is/are, prepositions, short reading, and a home description. |
| 15 | Module 2 Test: My People and My Place | Cumulative test | Complete a picture description, a family/home reading, contextual grammar, and a short written home profile. |

**Grammar depth for Module 2:** explain that **a/an** means one non-specific thing, while plural nouns usually need a number or another word. Explain that **there is** introduces one thing and **there are** introduces more than one. Include common Arabic-speaker mistakes such as “There have a table” and missing English plural endings. Do not present exceptions as a memorization wall; introduce common irregular plurals such as *child/children* as word-bank items.

**Core repeated exposure:** *number, age, family, mother, father, brother, sister, friend, home, room, kitchen, bedroom, table, chair, book, bag, red, blue, big, small, in, on, under, next to, my, your, his, her*.

## 6. Module 3 — Food, Fruit, Vegetables, and Everyday Choices

**Mentor arc:** “Food is a safe place to practice because you can see it, point to it, choose it, and share it. We will learn how English makes a simple choice clear.” Arabic support remains available for countable/uncountable ideas because the distinction does not map neatly onto every learner’s first-language intuition.

| L | Lesson title | Mode | New language and learner action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fruit on the Table | Visual vocabulary | Learn apple, banana, orange, lemon, grape, strawberry, watermelon, fruit, sweet, fresh. Identify and say the words. |
| 2 | Vegetables in the Kitchen | Visual vocabulary | Learn tomato, potato, onion, carrot, cucumber, lettuce, bean, vegetable, wash, cut. Sort pictures. |
| 3 | I Like and I Don’t Like | Grammar discovery | Use like, love, don’t like, hate with food. Explain positive and negative meaning through preference cards. |
| 4 | Do You Like…? | Controlled grammar | Form yes/no questions with **do** and short answers: “Do you like tea?” “Yes, I do / No, I don’t.” |
| 5 | Breakfast and Drinks | Vocabulary + dialogue | Learn bread, egg, cheese, rice, chicken, water, tea, coffee, milk, breakfast. Follow a breakfast conversation. |
| 6 | Ordering a Simple Meal | Speaking | Repeat and perform “I’d like…” and “Can I have…?” with polite responses. |
| 7 | Some, Any, and Quantity | Grammar discovery | Introduce some/any and a small set of quantity phrases: a lot of, a little, a few. Keep examples concrete. |
| 8 | The Market List | Reading | Read a 70–90 word shopping list and short market exchange. Find quantities and prices with visual support. |
| 9 | Countable and Uncountable Food | Grammar-to-context | Explain “one apple/two apples” versus “some rice/some water” using real pictures, not abstract definitions. |
| 10 | My Favourite Meal | Writing | Write 6–8 guided sentences about a meal, ingredients, likes, and dislikes. |
| 11 | At the Shop | Interaction | Ask for food, understand simple prices, say “anything else?”, answer, thank the shopkeeper, and close the interaction. |
| 12 | Word-Bank Retrieval 3 | Review | Retrieve food through picture naming, pronunciation, spelling, category sorting, preferences, quantities, and dialogue completion. |
| 13 | Plan a Shared Meal | Supported production | Choose a simple meal for two people and explain the choices using “We need…”, “I like…”, and “We have…” |
| 14 | Module 3 Milestone | Assessment | Food vocabulary, likes/questions, quantity, short market reading, listening to an order, and a guided meal description. |
| 15 | Module 3 Test: Food for Today | Cumulative test | Complete a shop role-play, a menu/list reading, contextual grammar, and a written meal plan. |

**Grammar depth for Module 3:** explain **do** as a helper used to make present-simple questions and negatives with many action verbs. Do not call it an “auxiliary” at first. Show that “Do you like?” is not translated word-for-word. Include Arabic-speaker guidance on word order and the need for **do** in “I don’t like…” rather than “I not like…”. Treat food quantity as meaning, not as a test of terminology.

**Core repeated exposure:** *apple, banana, orange, tomato, potato, onion, carrot, bread, egg, cheese, rice, chicken, water, tea, coffee, milk, breakfast, lunch, meal, like, love, don’t like, want, need, some, any, a lot of*.

## 7. Module 4 — Time, Daily Life, and Healthy Habits

**Mentor arc:** “You can now name your world. Next, we will place actions in time. English uses small words and endings to show what happens every day.” The mentor carefully separates **be** from action verbs so learners do not produce “I am go” or “I am eat” when the intended meaning is a routine.

| L | Lesson title | Mode | New language and learner action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Days, Months, and Today | Vocabulary | Learn days, today, tomorrow, morning, afternoon, evening, night, week, month. Sequence a calendar. |
| 2 | Telling the Time | Visual + speaking | Learn o’clock, half past, quarter past, quarter to, early, late. Say times with clock visuals. |
| 3 | My Morning | Vocabulary | Learn wake up, get up, wash, shower, brush, get dressed, eat, drink, go, leave. Order picture cards. |
| 4 | Present Simple for Routines | Grammar discovery | Use “I get up,” “You work,” and third-person examples only after first-person control. Explain a routine as something repeated. |
| 5 | He, She, and the **-s** Ending | Grammar | Introduce “He works / She likes” with a small set of common verbs. Explain the ending as an English signal, not a reason to fear speaking. |
| 6 | A Day in My Life | Speaking | Repeat and tell a six-step routine using time phrases and sequence words: first, then, after that, finally. |
| 7 | Always, Usually, Sometimes, Never | Grammar + vocabulary | Place frequency words in short sentences and compare two routines. |
| 8 | A Short Routine Text | Reading | Read an 80–100 word daily routine with times and familiar actions. Identify the order of events. |
| 9 | Do You…? Questions About Habits | Interaction | Ask and answer “Do you…?” questions about waking, eating, studying, and sleeping. |
| 10 | Healthy Choices | Vocabulary + grammar | Learn healthy, tired, hungry, thirsty, exercise, sleep, fruit, water, and simple advice with **should** in fixed, supported phrases. |
| 11 | My Week | Writing | Write 8–10 sentences about a weekday and one weekend habit using a scaffold. |
| 12 | Word-Bank Retrieval 4 | Review | Retrieve routine verbs, time, days, frequency, food, and health words through ordering, pronunciation, and contextual completion. |
| 13 | Compare Two Days | Supported production | Describe a normal day and a different day using “but,” “and,” and “then.” |
| 14 | Module 4 Milestone | Assessment | Time, present simple, third-person **-s**, frequency, routine reading, and a guided spoken routine. |
| 15 | Module 4 Test: A Day That Works | Cumulative test | Interpret a timetable, answer routine questions, give a short speaking account, and write a daily routine with a healthy choice. |

**Grammar depth for Module 4:** explain action verbs versus the connector **be** through contrast: “I am tired” versus “I work.” Teach the third-person ending through a small high-frequency set and explicitly state that learners can communicate before every irregular detail is mastered. Include Arabic-speaker guidance on subject-verb agreement, word order, and overusing **am** before action verbs.

**Core repeated exposure:** *day, week, morning, evening, today, time, wake up, get up, wash, eat, drink, go, work, study, sleep, always, usually, sometimes, never, healthy, tired, hungry, thirsty, should*.

## 8. Module 5 — People at Work, Places in Town, and Useful Services

**Mentor arc:** “English becomes useful when another person needs to understand you. We will practise asking for help, finding a place, and saying what people do.” The mentor models politeness and repair phrases rather than expecting perfect sentences.

| L | Lesson title | Mode | New language and learner action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jobs People Do | Visual vocabulary | Learn teacher, doctor, nurse, driver, farmer, cook, shop assistant, student, worker, office, school, hospital. |
| 2 | What Is the Job? | Grammar + vocabulary | Use “He is a doctor” and “She works in a school.” Distinguish identity from workplace with pictures. |
| 3 | Places in Town | Visual vocabulary | Learn bank, shop, market, station, bus stop, pharmacy, restaurant, park, mosque, street, town, city. |
| 4 | There Is a Place Here | Grammar | Recycle there is/are with town maps; use “There is a bank near the school.” |
| 5 | Asking Where | Interaction | Learn “Where is…?”, “Is there…?”, “How do I get to…?” with slow map-based dialogues. |
| 6 | Directions and Movement | Speaking | Repeat left, right, straight, near, far, across from, next to, turn, go, stop; perform a short direction exchange. |
| 7 | At the Pharmacy | Dialogue | Practise concrete needs: “I need medicine,” “I have a headache,” “How much is it?” without turning the lesson into medical advice. |
| 8 | A Town Notice | Reading | Read a 90–110 word notice about opening times, a bus stop, or a community event. Find dates, places, and times. |
| 9 | Can I Help You? | Interaction | Use can for ability and polite service requests: “Can you help me?” “Can I have…?” |
| 10 | Work and My Future | Writing | Write 8–10 supported sentences about a job someone has or a job the learner would like to learn about. |
| 11 | A Day at Work | Reading + speaking | Follow a short profile of a worker and retell three facts using picture prompts. |
| 12 | Word-Bank Retrieval 5 | Review | Retrieve jobs, places, directions, service phrases, times, prices, and can/can’t through map and dialogue tasks. |
| 13 | Solve a Small Problem | Supported production | Choose one of three situations: wrong bus, closed shop, or missing item. Use repair phrases and a simple request. |
| 14 | Module 5 Milestone | Assessment | Job/place vocabulary, there is/are, directions, can, service dialogue, and notice reading. |
| 15 | Module 5 Test: Around My Town | Cumulative test | Complete a map interaction, interpret a town notice, answer contextual grammar questions, and write a short service message. |

**Grammar depth for Module 5:** explain **can** as a small word used for ability and requests. Demonstrate that it does not change with he/she and does not take **to** before the next verb in basic patterns: “I can help,” not “I can to help.” Teach polite requests as complete chunks before analyzing them.

**Core repeated exposure:** *teacher, doctor, nurse, driver, cook, school, hospital, office, shop, market, bank, station, pharmacy, park, street, near, far, left, right, straight, help, need, can, turn, go*.

## 9. Module 6 — Hobbies, Sports, Weather, and Real-Life Interaction

**Mentor arc:** “You now have enough English to enter a small conversation. This final A1 module helps you say what you enjoy, respond to another person, make a simple plan, and recover when communication is difficult.” This module consolidates rather than introducing a large new grammar burden.

| L | Lesson title | Mode | New language and learner action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free-Time Activities | Visual vocabulary | Learn read, write, watch, listen, play, cook, walk, visit, music, film, book, game. |
| 2 | Sports and Movement | Visual vocabulary | Learn football, tennis, swimming, running, cycling, team, ball, win, lose, practice. |
| 3 | I Like Doing… | Grammar discovery | Use like + noun and like + simple activity chunk: “I like music,” “I like reading.” Keep the explanation example-led. |
| 4 | Weather Today | Vocabulary | Learn sunny, rainy, cloudy, windy, hot, cold, warm, weather, spring, summer, winter, autumn. |
| 5 | What Are You Doing? | Grammar | Introduce a carefully bounded present-continuous contrast: “I am reading now” versus “I read every day.” |
| 6 | Repeat a Short Conversation | Speaking | Perform a conversation about hobbies, weather, and what is happening now. Use replay and self-recording. |
| 7 | Invitations and Plans | Interaction | Learn “Would you like…?”, “Do you want to…?”, “Yes, please,” “Sorry, I can’t,” and “Maybe another day.” |
| 8 | A Simple Event Poster | Reading | Read a 100–120 word poster or message with date, time, place, activity, and simple invitation. |
| 9 | Reply to a Message | Writing | Write a 5–7 sentence response accepting or declining a simple invitation and giving one reason. |
| 10 | Fixing Communication | Interaction | Practise “Please say that again,” “Please speak slowly,” “What does ___ mean?”, and “I mean…” |
| 11 | My Favourite Activity | Supported production | Give a one-minute guided talk with a picture sequence, three reasons, and one question for the listener. |
| 12 | Word-Bank Retrieval 6 | Review | Retrieve hobbies, sports, weather, invitations, repair language, and all prior module categories. |
| 13 | A1 Survival Conversation | Integrated practice | Complete a six-stage scenario: greeting, introduction, choice, request, clarification, and goodbye. |
| 14 | A1 Final Milestone | Assessment | Complete a listening dialogue, short reading, contextual grammar, guided speaking, and a short written reply. |
| 15 | A1 Exit Test: Use Your English | Cumulative test | Complete a practical interaction, read a familiar text, write a 10–12 sentence supported passage, and review the learner’s strongest and next-needed skills. |

**Grammar depth for Module 6:** explain the difference between a routine and something happening now through paired pictures. Do not require mastery of every present-continuous spelling rule at A1. Teach the forms **I am**, **you are**, **he/she is** + activity as reusable frames. Consolidate questions, negatives, **can**, present simple, and **be** rather than adding unrelated structures.

**Core repeated exposure:** *read, write, watch, listen, play, music, film, book, game, football, swim, run, weather, sunny, rainy, hot, cold, today, now, like, want, invite, meet, again, slowly, mean*.

## 10. Grammar syllabus and teaching standard

The following grammar sequence is the maximum intended A1 scope. A topic may appear in multiple modules, but the first encounter should be concrete and the later encounters should increase control.

| Grammar topic | First introduction | Required teaching content |
|---|---:|---|
| English sentence building | M1 L3 | Person/topic + verb + information; why English usually needs an expressed subject and present **be**. |
| **Be**: am/is/are | M1 L3 | Meaning, positive, negative, questions, short answers, contractions after full forms, Arabic-speaker errors. |
| Pronouns | M1 L3 | I, you, he, she, it, we, they through people and objects; subject versus ownership kept concrete. |
| Articles and singular/plural | M2 L3 | a/an, one/more than one, regular plural, common irregular words, spelling and pronunciation. |
| Demonstratives | M2 L4 | this/that/these/those with physical distance and number. |
| Possessives | M2 L7 | my, your, his, her, our, their; “the name of…” only as optional support. |
| There is/are | M2 L9 | Introduce and describe one or more objects; positive, negative, questions in simple contexts. |
| Present simple | M4 L4 | Repeated routines; positive with I/you/we/they; basic questions and negatives with do. |
| Third-person **-s** | M4 L5 | He/she/it forms with common verbs; meaning before spelling rules. |
| Frequency | M4 L7 | always, usually, sometimes, never in short routine statements. |
| Imperatives | M1 L11 | Classroom and direction commands; positive and simple “don’t” commands. |
| **Can** | M5 L9 | Ability and basic requests; positive, negative, questions; no third-person change. |
| Count and quantity | M3 L7/L9 | some/any, a lot of, a little, a few, food examples, concrete countability. |
| Present continuous | M6 L5 | “Happening now” in simple paired examples; do not overload with full spelling taxonomy. |
| Basic prepositions | M2 L9/M5 L6 | in, on, under, next to, behind, near, across from, left/right. |
| Connectors | M4 L6 onward | and, but, then, because in very short connected texts. |

Every grammar record should contain: a plain-language meaning; why the learner needs it; a visual structure; positive, negative, and question forms where relevant; short answers; at least six English examples with Arabic translations; when to use it; at least two common Arabic-speaker mistakes; a note about exceptions or limits; controlled practice; guided production; and one real-world application.

The Arabic explanation should not be a word-for-word translation of an English grammar textbook. It should explain the communication problem. For example, for **be**: “في الإنجليزية نحتاج عادةً إلى فعل يربط الشخص بالصفة أو الاسم في الزمن الحاضر: I am happy = أنا سعيد. لا نحذف am كما يحدث أحياناً في العربية.” The platform should then return to English practice immediately.

## 11. Repeated-exposure and word-bank architecture

Each module owns a continuously updating word bank. A word-bank entry must contain the fields below. The source lesson is retained permanently so the learner can return to the original explanation rather than seeing an orphaned flashcard.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| English item | apple |
| Arabic meaning | تفاحة |
| Simple meaning | A round fruit that can be red, green, or yellow. |
| Pronunciation | /ˈæpəl/ plus a readable phonetic respelling where useful |
| Audio state | Browser playback initially; replaceable by approved British audio asset later |
| Example sentence | I eat an apple in the morning. |
| Arabic example | آكل تفاحة في الصباح. |
| Source lesson | A1-M3-L1 |
| Exposure history | learned → picture → grammar → dialogue → reading → writing → quiz → review |
| Learner status | new, learning, familiar, needs review, secure |

A vocabulary anchor should normally appear in at least six planned exposures after introduction. The exposure modes should be meaningful rather than duplicated question cards: recognition, pronunciation, grammar frame, dialogue, reading, writing, contextual quiz, and spaced review. High-value items such as *name*, *family*, *like*, *want*, *need*, *go*, *food*, and *time* should cross module boundaries and be marked as cumulative vocabulary.

A word-bank review session should mix English-to-Arabic recognition, Arabic-to-English retrieval, picture naming, listening discrimination, sentence completion, spelling of known words, and personal sentence production. Spelling tasks must never present intentionally misspelled distractors as if they were acceptable English. Contextual distractors should be real correctly spelled words with a meaningful relationship to the sentence.

## 12. Assessment architecture

Assessment is distributed. A learner should receive useful feedback before a high-stakes gate, and the gate should measure whether the learner can use the language rather than whether they remember the visual appearance of a lesson page.

| Assessment | Frequency | Contents | Learner result |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Micro-check | Lessons 2–13 | 3–6 items, one target pattern or word family, immediate explanation. | Continue or review one item. |
| Lesson quiz | End of teaching lesson | 8 contextual questions drawn from the lesson bank, including vocabulary and grammar. | Pass at 80%; retry after review. |
| Retrieval review | Lessons 7, 12, and 13 | Mixed old/new word-bank retrieval and pronunciation. | Personal review recommendations. |
| Milestone checkpoint | Lesson 14 each module | Recent module content plus one integrated reading/speaking/writing task. | Skill profile, not the only gate. |
| Module test | Lesson 15 each module | Cumulative vocabulary, grammar-in-context, reading, practical interaction, and short writing. | 80% objective threshold plus task completion to unlock the next module. |
| A1 exit test | Module 6 Lesson 15 | Integrated practical communication across all modules. | A1 skill profile and recommended bridge to A2. |

Each new attempt should vary the question selection and context while preserving the same learning target. A question such as “Choose the correct continuation: I would like…” should use realistic options such as “some water,” “a table,” or “to sit here,” depending on the lesson context. It should not rely on obvious misspellings or unrelated distractors.

## 13. Normal learner session

The tutorial should explain the following flow in plain English with Arabic support. First, the learner opens the next unlocked lesson. The mentor explains what the lesson is for. The learner meets a small set of words and hears them. The learner sees pictures or examples, notices one grammar pattern, and practises it in controlled steps. The learner then enters a dialogue, repeats useful lines, reads a short text, writes a small response, completes the lesson quiz, and reviews any missed word-bank items. The learner does not need to complete every task perfectly before moving within a lesson, but must pass the lesson gate before the next lesson opens.

The learner should always be able to answer three questions: **Where am I? What am I practising now? What will I do next?** The sidebar should show module, lesson number, type, completion state, and gate state. The word bank should be one click away. The Guide button should reopen the tutorial on mobile and desktop. External-AI prompt features should clearly explain that the learner copies the prompt to an external service and returns with the result; the website should not imply that it has silently graded the response if it has not.

## 14. Migration acceptance criteria

This specification should not be considered implemented until the active catalog visibly satisfies all of the following conditions:

| Acceptance test | Required result |
|---|---|
| Active A1 module count | Six named modules visible to the learner. |
| Active lesson count | Fifteen active lesson records per A1 module, for 90 active A1 lessons. |
| Lesson variety | Every module contains vocabulary, visual, grammar, dialogue, speaking, reading, writing, review, milestone, and cumulative assessment lessons. |
| Provenance | Every active lesson points to a stable authored source key and source lesson. |
| Gates | Lesson and module progression still obeys the existing pass requirements. |
| Word bank | Every module’s word bank includes source lesson and exposure history. |
| Assessment | Every module has active milestone and cumulative question banks with correct module/checkpoint provenance. |
| Learner map | The learner sees the new six-module structure and 15 lessons per module, not a preview notice alongside five legacy lessons. |
| Owner review | The owner can inspect every A1 lesson before or after migration and see its grammar, activities, vocabulary, and assessment content. |
| Responsive behavior | The 15-lesson module structure remains navigable on mobile with independently scrolling navigation. |

## 15. Visual-system consistency standard

The expanded A1 curriculum must **feel like the same English Journey product**, not a separate curriculum bolted onto the existing site. The migration will reuse the established visual language: the deep navy learning-space shell, warm gold progression accents, paper-toned content cards, rounded learning surfaces, DM Sans and Noto Sans Arabic typography, visible focus rings, and the existing compact mobile navigation rhythm. New lesson types may introduce appropriate visual material, but they must compose from these existing tokens rather than invent a competing palette, spacing scale, button style, or icon language.

| Surface | Required consistency rule | A1-specific addition |
|---|---|---|
| Learner map | Preserve the existing module-card hierarchy, progress state, locked/available treatment, and bilingual title layout. | Show all 15 active lesson cards in the same grid and use a compact type badge rather than a preview-only panel. |
| Lesson workspace | Keep the mentor-led sequence, card rhythm, heading scale, primary action treatment, and Arabic support placement. | Change activity content by lesson type, not the surrounding page shell or navigation model. |
| Word bank | Retain current review controls, card behavior, pronunciation affordance, and status language. | Add source lesson and exposure-history metadata as secondary information, never as clutter before the word itself. |
| Tutorial and prompts | Preserve the guided modal/drawer format, bilingual hierarchy, and concise step framing. | Explain the 15-lesson rhythm and lesson-type badges using the same mentor voice and task cards. |
| Owner review | Retain inspection-oriented tabs, neutral cards, and clear loaded/empty states. | Present authored source keys and migration status as compact metadata, not a second dashboard visual system. |
| Mobile | Preserve independent sidebar scrolling, collapsible navigation, touch targets, and clear next-step calls to action. | Use one-column lesson cards and expandable details; do not rely on hover-only explanations or dense horizontal tables. |

Before release, desktop and mobile screenshots must be reviewed together for the learner map, a visual-vocabulary lesson, a grammar lesson, a dialogue/speaking lesson, a reading/writing lesson, the module word bank, the tutorial, and owner review. A new A1 surface fails this standard if it uses unpaired background/text tokens, loses Arabic readability, creates a new navigation pattern, hides keyboard focus, or makes a 15-lesson module harder to scan than the current five-lesson card.

## Review questions for the owner

Please approve or revise the following before implementation: whether six modules is the right A1 breadth; whether fifteen lessons per module is the right minimum; whether the six themes match the intended learner journey; whether the proposed 90-lesson volume is acceptable for an A1 track; whether the module test should require both an 80% objective score and completion of a short production task; and whether the Arabic support should reduce after Module 2 or remain equally visible throughout A1.
